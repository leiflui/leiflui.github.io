Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals: accessing generic orbits

Ondřej Zelenka, Michal Stratený, Petr Kotlařík, Georgios Lukes-Gerakopoulos

Gravitational waves EMRIs Teukolsky

2611.30414v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 23 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 23 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals (EMRI) are a significant gravitational wave source and a target for the upcoming LISA mission. Computations of the fluxes of conserved quantities, such as energy and angular momentum, are a critical part of modeling EMRI waveforms and can be performed using a frequency domain formalism to solve the Teukolsky equation. This requires computations of large numbers of mode amplitudes, many of which are negligible. However, it is unknown ex ante which can be safely skipped. We present a machine learning approach to estimate the dominant radial mode number as a function of the remaining mode numbers and apply it to reduce the number of unnecessary computations in EMRI flux calculation.

arXiv abstract · PDF at arXiv

AI summary and assessment

This proof-of-concept study uses a neural network to predict which radial harmonic dominates a generic-orbit EMRI flux calculation. Frequency-domain Teukolsky computations supply labels for combinations of orbital parameters and mode indices. A nonlinear network substantially outperforms a model linear in the mode numbers: rounded predictions match 88.6% of test labels exactly and 98.5% within one harmonic. The intended benefit is to locate promising modes before spending time on negligible contributions. However, the reported tests split mode samples from the same orbits across training and evaluation, and all backgrounds have spin 0.9. The paper therefore establishes an encouraging mode-selection estimator, rather than a validated general-purpose flux accelerator or a demonstrated waveform-accuracy guarantee.

The Geometric Imprint of a Tilted Accretion Disk in Gravitational Waves

Jing-Ze Xia, Chen Yuan

Gravitational waves GRMHD Teukolsky

2611.23914v1 · astro-ph.HE, gr-qc

First submitted: 18 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 18 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; GRMHD; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

Gravitational wave astronomy has opened a new window onto black holes and the dynamics of strong-field gravity, while accretion flows offer a complementary and natural laboratory for probing strong-field physics. In this work, we show that the gravitational wave emission from an initially tilted accretion disk carries a distinct imprint of its orientation. We perform three dimensional general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations and evaluate the gravitational waves through Teukolsky equation. For an aligned disk, the emission is concentrated in low-$|m|$ modes, where $m$ is the azimuthal quantum number. For the initially tilted disk, higher-$|m|$ modes make a larger relative contribution. As the tilted flow subsequently aligns with the black hole spin, the contribution of $|m|\ge2$ modes decreases, showing strong correlation with the tilt angle. This indicates that the gravitational waves generated by the black hole environment carries geometric information about the surrounding matter. Our results establish a route towards using multimode gravitational radiation to probe the geometry and dynamical evolution of accretion flows in the strong-field region.

arXiv abstract · PDF at arXiv

AI summary and assessment

The study asks whether gravitational radiation from an accretion flow records the disk’s tilt relative to a spinning black hole. Two matched three-dimensional GRMHD simulations, initially aligned or tilted by 75 degrees, supply stress–energy sources for time-domain Teukolsky calculations on a fixed Kerr background. The tilted flow contributes more power to higher azimuthal modes; as it aligns, the high-mode fraction declines alongside the measured tilt. Mode-cutoff checks and MRI resolution diagnostics support the numerical example, while a rotated-axis model provides a geometric interpretation. This connects disk dynamics to a potentially useful multimode gravitational-wave signature. Generality and observability remain open: only two initial orientations and one spin are studied, and the illustrative strain rescaling is not a detector-inference analysis.

Complete quasinormal modes of Type-D black holes. III. Reissner-Nordström spectra from zero to extremal charge

Changkai Chen, Xiaohua Zhang, Zhoujian Cao, Jiliang Jing

Resonance Quasi-normal modes

2611.24647v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 18 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 23 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

Earlier calculations left open how gravitational quasinormal modes (QNMs) of charged black holes recover the Schwarzschild spectrum and how the modes near algebraically special frequencies relate to the exact special solutions. We extend our Kerr analysis to Reissner-Nordström black holes by analytically continuing the ingoing horizon solution and locating the zeros of the incoming amplitude at infinity. For each of $\ell=2$ and $3$, we trace the first 42 overtone families from the Schwarzschild limit to extremality. The charge trajectories distinguish crossings of the negative imaginary axis from turns near it and separate the unconventional Schwarzschild modes from neighboring branches. At zero charge the polar algebraically special mode is a QNM. At extremal charge the exact axial special modes are total-transmission modes. Of the 84 families, 75 approach distinct extremal frequencies with finite damping, and nine approach zero frequency with vanishing excitation. At exact extremality we also compute, for the first time, gravitational QNMs on the unconventional Riemann sheet reached across the negative imaginary axis. At intermediate charge, the clearest resonant enhancement of both excitation factors occurs for the $\ell=3$ overtone pairs $(22,23)$ and $(23,24)$.

Modeling the gravitational wave spectrum from slowly-evolving sources in the early Universe: constant-in-time and coherent-decay models

Antonino S. Midiri, Alberto Roper Pol, Madeline Salomé, Chiara Caprini

Gravitational waves Resonance

2611.26576v1 · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc

First submitted: 19 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 19 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

We study the production of gravitational wave (GW) backgrounds from slowly-evolving sources in the early Universe, following the Constant-in-Time (CiT) model, presented and validated against simulations of decaying turbulence in arXiv:2201.05630. We extend the CiT model to account for the source decay using a coherent description for the unequal-time correlator (UETC) of the source. We present a generic framework that allows us to study different types of sources. As examples, we discuss the applicability of this framework to the production of GWs from decaying MHD turbulence, sound waves, and bubble collisions in first-order phase transitions. We show that the validity of the CiT and the coherent-decay models presented in this work relies on long decorrelation times of the source. However, as the decorrelation timescale of the UETC becomes short, a resonant term grows on top of the GW spectrum predicted by the CiT model, as found, for example, in earlier analytical studies of GW production from turbulence and sound waves. This model is compatible with previous numerical results and explains why sound waves in a radiation-dominated fluid are more efficient than subrelativistic MHD turbulence in producing GWs, which is no longer the case for relativistic turbulence. The CiT model predicts a generic spectrum that scales as $k^3$ in the IR regime, develops a linear $k$ at intermediate frequencies, and decays as $k^{1-b_Π}$ at frequencies above the inverse correlation length scale of the source, where $b_Π$ is uniquely determined by the type of source and the UV slope of its spectrum. Since the $k^3$ and $k$ scalings always appear for slowly-evolving sources and they are commonly found in studies of GW production, we motivate checking the potential applicability of the CiT to other sources of GWs.

Fuzzy Dark Matter as a diffractive barrier: Wave-optics Signatures of Ultralight Fields on Lensed Gravitational Waves

Manish Tamta, Han Gil Choi, Diego Blas, Miguel Zumalacárregui

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing

2611.23580v1 · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph

First submitted: 17 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 17 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

Gravitational waves (GWs) propagate through the universe from sources at high redshift, experiencing the gravitational fields of the intervening matter distribution. Halos composed of ultralight dark matter (ULDM) contain strong density fluctuations, reflecting the interference pattern in the field distribution of the ultralight bosons. In the wave-optics regime, ULDM fluctuations act as a diffractive grating, imprinting frequency-dependent modulations of GWs, in addition to magnification and other gravitational lensing effects. Since lensing depends on the projected surface density, we derive its approximate probability distribution and show how it departs from a Gaussian distribution depending on the halo profile and coherence length. We then quantify how the GW diffraction signatures produced by the granules depend on macroimage magnification and parity. The ULDM interference network imprints stochastic diffraction fluctuations at the $O(1)\%$ level on GWs moderately lensed by the host when the Fresnel scale is comparable to the granule coherence length $r_F(f)\sim\ell_c$. These fluctuations can be enhanced to $\gtrsim10\%$ when the host macroimage forms near the critical curve or for Type II macroimages. For massive black hole binaries observed by LISA, the diffraction signature can be detectable around the ULDM particle mass $m_φ\sim10^{-21}$ eV, providing either a smoking-gun signature of ULDM or new constraints on $m_φ$.

Convergence of the quasinormal-mode expansion of the Kerr Green function

Davide Ciattaglia, Adrien Kuntz, Enrico Barausse

Gravitational waves Quasi-normal modes

2611.23110v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, hep-th

First submitted: 17 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 17 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

Black-hole ringdown is usually modeled as a linear superposition of quasinormal modes, and fits to gravitational-wave observations increasingly include high overtones. However, the overtone sum does not necessarily converge: overtones are more strongly damped and thus decay faster in time, but their amplitudes in the Green function (the signal at an observer produced by an impulse at a source point) grow, so the mode description may break down before a finite time. We show that for a rotating (Kerr) black hole this time has a clear geometric meaning. For highly damped modes, the amplitudes grow exponentially with the optical distance from the source and the observer to a characteristic "bounce" radius close to the black hole, defined as the location where the mode amplitude is smallest. For one family of modes, the amplitudes are also suppressed the farther the source and observer sit from the equatorial plane. Rotation therefore makes the onset of convergence depend on direction as well as distance, extending the geometric picture from bounce radii to bounce surfaces. Combining these effects gives a sufficient condition for absolute convergence. For spin $a/M = 0.7$, we map how this time varies with source and observer position, and amplitudes computed numerically reproduce the predicted spatial dependence to better than one percent. These results set geometric limits on when the quasinormal-mode sums converge.

arXiv abstract · PDF at arXiv

AI summary and assessment

It is not a complete representation of the causal response: nonpole contributions remain necessary, and an extended physical source requires integration over its emitting history and position.

Beyond the point lens: radial structure and the horizon in wave-optics gravitational lensing

Alice Garoffolo, Gianmassimo Tasinato

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing

2611.22618v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, hep-th

First submitted: 17 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 17 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

Gravitational-wave lensing in the wave-optics regime is sensitive to the structure of the lens beyond its total mass: to its exterior radial profile and, as we show, to the boundary condition at its centre. We develop an analytic framework to compute these effects for a scalar wave, as corrections to the wave lensed by a Newtonian point mass. The corrections are organized in inverse powers of the distance from the lens and computed with dressed radial kernels, which include the Newtonian interaction to all orders and resum the long-range phase that invalidates the Born expansion around the free wave. Retaining wavefront curvature, we find that the scattered wave is not the whole response: an inverse-square deformation changes the direct wave by an equal amount, even when the scattered wave is faint. Steeper terms of the exterior potential lead to short-distance divergences, which signal that the exterior geometry alone does not determine the lensed wave: the missing information is a set of local terms fixed by the inner boundary condition. For a Schwarzschild black hole we determine them by matching to the ingoing solution at the horizon. The horizon enters the amplification factor as an absorptive contact term, which reproduces the horizon-area cross section; it is absent for a non-absorbing lens of the same mass, and cannot be described by the thin-lens diffraction integral. It completes the correction quadratic in the mass, where it appears at the same order as finite-distance optical corrections, and largely cancels against them for an aligned source. Together, these results connect Newtonian dressing, diffraction and inner boundary conditions, in a systematic description of corrections to point-mass gravitational-wave lensing.

Detectability of Eccentric Intermediate Mass-Ratio Inspiral in the Decihertz Band

Devesh Giri, Sajal Mukherjee, Apratim Ganguly, Sanjit Mitra

Gravitational waves Black-hole perturbation theory Teukolsky

2611.21323v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM, hep-th

First submitted: 16 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 16 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Black-hole perturbation theory; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

The proposed decihertz gravitational-wave (GW) band $(\sim 10^{-2}-1\,\Hz)$ will observe the late inspiral of intermediate mass-ratio inspirals (IMRIs), binaries with mass ratios $10^2 \lesssim q \lesssim 10^4$ that assemble dynamically in dense stellar environments and are expected to retain measurable eccentricity. Search and parameter-estimation pipelines are predominantly built around circular templates, and we test this assumption. Using black-hole perturbation theory waveforms from \textsc{FastEMRIWaveforms} for a representative IMRI ($M=10^4\,\modot$, $q=100$, observed for its final $10\,\hr$), we compute fitting factors between eccentric signals and circular templates, maximizing over the template's total mass and mass ratio with a differential-evolution search, for the B-DECIGO, LGWA-Si, and IndIGO-D noise curves and a flat PSD. The mismatch grows with initial eccentricity $e_0$ and crosses the standard $3\%$ threshold at $e_0\simeq3\times10^{-2}$, well within the range allowed by the waveform model and expected from dynamical formation. The best-fitting circular templates also bias the total mass and mass ratio by several percent at $e_0=0.1$. Combining the mismatch with the signal-to-noise-dependent distinguishability criterion, we find that for a source at $1\,\mathrm{Gpc}$, eccentricities as small as $e_0\simeq5\times10^{-4}$ (B-DECIGO and IndIGO-D) and $5\times10^{-3}$ (LGWA-Si) are resolvable, one to two orders of magnitude below where detection efficiency degrades. The loss of match arises from the excitation of radial Teukolsky harmonics, which spread power into sidebands that circular templates cannot represent, and from the slower orbital decay of eccentric binaries at fixed initial semi-latus rectum. Robust detection and parameter estimation of IMRIs with DECIGO, LGWA, and IndIGO-D will require eccentric waveform models and template banks.

Constraining Gamma-Ray Emission Beyond the TOV Limit: A Targeted Fermi-GBM Search for Counterparts to 39 LVK Gravitational-Wave Candidates in GWTC-5.0

Navya Raj

Gravitational waves

2611.30800v1 · astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 23 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 23 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves

Read the author-provided abstract

We present a systematic search for sub-threshold gamma-ray counterparts within a one-day time window of 161 gravitational-wave (GW) events from the GWTC-5.0 catalog, whose inferred source masses exceed the adopted Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff (TOV) limit, using the Gamma-ray Targeted Search (GTS) pipeline. The search identifies 39 candidate GW--gamma-ray pairs satisfying the adopted joint likelihood-ratio thresholds. None of these candidates exhibits a temporal offset compatible with the prompt-emission delay observed for GW170817 ($\sim 1.7$ s); the smallest temporal separation is 17 min 17.976 s, ruling out GW170817-like prompt temporal associations in our candidate sample. Several candidates nevertheless show substantial spatial consistency. In particular, the most spatially consistent pair has a joint localization overlap of 95.4%, despite a temporal separation of 13 h 19 min 8.85 s, illustrating that high spatial agreement can occur by chance in the absence of temporal consistency. Among the sub-threshold gamma-ray candidates, we further identify an anomalous event with an exceptionally large single-instrument significance of log LR = 5250.84. For comparison, the adopted $3σ$ threshold corresponds to log LR $\approx 9$. The extreme likelihood ratio of this candidate motivates further investigation of trigger sensitivity, onboard veto configurations, and the statistical interpretation of sub-threshold events. Overall, we find no physically viable multi-messenger associations between the GWTC-5.0 events and the searched gamma-ray candidates, and derive corresponding upper limits on prompt high-energy emission from GWTC-5.0 progenitors.

Geometric distinguishability of two-source gravitational-wave signals from the extrinsic geometry of the single-source waveform manifold

Cezar Valentin Ionescu, Paul Adrian Gogîţă, Laurentiu Ioan Caramete, Ana Caramete

Gravitational waves

2611.30560v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 23 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 23 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves

Read the author-provided abstract

Overlapping gravitational-wave signals can produce strong degeneracies in which a multi-source waveform is accurately represented by a single effective source. We formulate this source-confusion problem geometrically by treating the family of single-source waveforms as an embedded manifold in the noise-weighted detector Hilbert space. We introduce the distance-to-manifold statistic, defined as the minimum residual obtained by fitting a waveform with the single-source model, and show that its squared value is directly related to the maximized likelihood difference between two-source and single-source hypotheses in the noiseless limit. For two sources with nearby parameters, perturbative minimization demonstrates that the effective single-source fit absorbs all leading tangent-space deformations. The first irreducible residual lies in the normal space of the waveform manifold and is governed by its second fundamental form. We validate our approach using a six-parameter frequency-domain inspiral model with a LISA-like time-delay-interferometry response. Direct optimization reproduces the predicted quartic scaling over the numerically resolved regime, while two-dimensional confusion-zone maps reveal strongly anisotropic distinguishability boundaries. These results establish extrinsic waveform-manifold curvature as a coordinate-independent diagnostic of local multi-source confusion and provide a practical framework for identifying parameter-space directions in which overlapping gravitational-wave sources are most difficult to resolve.

Read the author-provided abstract

We present analytical expressions for the energy and angular momentum fluxes radiated to infinity by a (nonspinning) point mass in an eccentric-equatorial orbit about a Kerr black hole. These results build upon our previous work involving point masses on eccentric orbits about a Schwarzschild black hole. We now adopt the Teukolsky formalism in first-order black hole perturbation theory to extend our methods to Kerr. Read the author-provided abstract

The ringdown of a perturbed black hole (BH) can be described as a superposition of quasinormal modes (QNMs), whose frequencies are determined by the spacetime geometry while their amplitudes depend also on the perturbing source. However, the physical mechanism governing mode excitation remains unclear and is typically treated on a case by case basis. In this work, we show that QNM excitation is governed by a simple spectral rule: each mode is excited according to the Fourier content of the perturbation evaluated at its characteristic frequency. This result follows from the factorization of the excitation coefficients and establishes a direct, quantitative connection between the spectral properties of the perturbation and the resulting ringdown amplitudes. To make this mechanism explicit and controllable, we construct localized perturbations with independently tunable spectral bandwidth and carrier frequency. We demonstrate analytically and numerically that BHs act as resonant spectral filters. We show analytically that the excitation amplitude of each mode equals the weighted spatial Fourier transform of the initial data evaluated at wavenumber $k\simω_n$ so that the filter selectively excites modes whose frequencies lie within the spectral support of the perturbation while suppressing others. Consequently, the excitation is maximized when the dominant perturbation frequency lies close to the real part of the QNM frequency, and we validate this at the percent level with fits to time-domain numerical evolutions. To robustly perform these fits, we have developed a new fitting algorithm, $\mathtt{QNMToolkit}$, which performs ringdown fits over large ensembles of sliding time-domain windows and quantifies the resulting fitting variance.

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves

Read the author-provided abstract

Mapping anisotropies in the gravitational-wave background (GWB) requires choosing a basis to represent the sky intensity, such as pixel and spherical harmonic bases. Each introduces a truncation---e.g., a number of pixels or a maximum multipole l_max---often set by a naive counting argument that relate the number of measurable modes to the number of independent cross-correlations, N_pair, in a pulsar timing array (PTA). However, such truncations can lead to reconstruction artifacts, as they do not reflect the true information content of the PTA response. Increasing the value of truncation parameters spans the observable space better but reveals poorly constrained modes, making the inverse problem ill conditioned and requiring regularization. A natural approach is to restrict the reconstruction to a well-measured subspace via principal maps, defined by the dominant eigenmodes of the detector response (or Fisher) matrix. However, these maps are not a fundamental parametrization of the sky but, rather, are derived from an underlying representation---such as a pixelization or a spherical harmonic expansion. While their explicit form depends on basis choice, they can span the \textit{same subspace} when the underlying representation is sufficiently complete. Here, we show that reconstructed anisotropy maps via different bases are equivalent, provided they retain the same information content, i.e., span the same principal subspace. As illustrative cases, we consider a toy model for PTA configuration and several GWB anisotropy shapes: point source, an extended source with deterministic anisotropy, and a statistical isotropic background, along with its summary statistic---the angular power spectrum. Although the reconstructions are equivalent, their computational costs can differ. We conclude with brief comments on the construction of principal maps for ground-based interferometers.
