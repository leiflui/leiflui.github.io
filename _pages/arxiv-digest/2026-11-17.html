Polarization of strongly lensed gravitational waves as a probe of the lens-induced orientations of electromagnetic counterparts

Kyungmin Kim

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing

2611.17637v1 · astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 13 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 13 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

Strong gravitational lensing is a well-established phenomenon in electromagnetic (EM) observations, where the lensed images and arcs are resolved on the two-dimensional sky and constrain the mass distribution of the lens. Strongly lensed gravitational waves (GWs) are also expected to be detected routinely by next-generation detectors, but their multiple images are recorded only as one-dimensional time series without positions. Among them, lensed binary neutron star mergers can be accompanied by EM counterparts with resolvable structures, such as relativistic jets. In EM lensing, the lens-induced change in the apparent orientation of such a structure can be measured only relative to other images or statistically, because its intrinsic orientation is not directly observable. In this work, we show that the GW polarization, which the lens leaves unchanged, provides this missing reference, i.e., a compass, so that the orientation offset of each image can be measured in absolute terms. We place this orientation layer on the EM image plane together with image positions from the lensed host arcs and Fermat-potential heights from GW time delays and magnifications. Using a mock population of such mergers observable by the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer, we find that the predicted image positions and arrival times are calibrated once the detection selection is included. The lens-induced orientation offsets reach tens of degrees and are detectable in up to a third of the events, at viewing angles above about $40^\circ$ for quadrupole-dominated signals.

Lense-Thirring induced repeating blast trains in continuous gravitational waves

Kailib Ryan Doney, Souvik Jana, Otto A. Hannuksela, Paul Martens

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing

2611.16914v1 · astro-ph.HE, gr-qc

First submitted: 12 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 12 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

We characterise gravitational lensing induced by the Lense-Thirring effect in a continuous gravitational-wave source orbiting a spinning supermassive black hole. This scenario is very likely to generate lensing events, as long as the source's orbital inclination is not aligned with the black hole's spin axis and the Lense-Thirring timescale is shorter than the observational window. The signal appears as short-duration ($T_{\rm blast}$) gravitational-wave "blasts" that repeat after every orbit ($T_{\rm orb}$) within a limited time window ($T_{\rm train}$). The blasts' signal strength depends on the system's geometry and not on the black hole mass. The signal will go silent and renew twice in a full Lense-Thirring precession period.

Degeneracies in the quasinormal mode spectrum of the Kerr-Sen black hole

Sérgio V. M. C. B. Xavier, João Paulo Cavalcante, Enzo Boschilia Vita, Maurício Richartz

Quasi-normal modes Black-hole perturbation theory

2611.15932v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 11 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 11 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Black-hole perturbation theory

Read the author-provided abstract

The non-Hermitian nature of the eigenvalue problem underlying black hole perturbations can induce the presence of exceptional points (EPs) in the quasinormal mode (QNM) spectrum. EPs are points in parameter space where QNMs become degenerate. Near an EP, QNMs are resonantly excited, leaving imprints on the ringdown signal. In this work, we investigate the exceptional structure of scalar perturbations of the Kerr-Sen black hole, a rotating, charged solution of the low-energy limit of heterotic string theory, and compare it with the EP structure of the Kerr-Newman black hole in General Relativity. Starting from the EP associated with a massive scalar field in the Kerr limit, we trace exceptional lines in the parameter space spanned by the black hole spin, charge, and field mass. We locate these degeneracies directly by imposing double-root conditions on the QNM secular equation, thus avoiding the need for parameter space scans. We also investigate the hysteresis phenomenon, showing that encircling an EP in parameter space leads to an exchange of the associated QNMs.

Eccentricity detectability in Extreme Mass-Ratio Inspirals

Maria Gonçalves Salgado, Lorenzo Speri, Elena M. Rossi

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2611.15736v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM

First submitted: 11 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 11 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

Extreme mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) consist of a stellar-mass compact object orbiting a massive black hole and are among the primary targets for the future space-based gravitational-wave detector LISA. Their orbital eccentricity carries valuable information about their astrophysical origins, making its detection particularly important. However, increasing eccentricity has competing effects on detectability: it makes eccentric waveforms easier to distinguish from circular ones, but also redistributes signal power across multiple harmonics, reducing the power captured by single-harmonic searches. In this work, we quantify this trade-off within a semi-coherent framework for a source with fixed total SNR of $50$. Eccentricity becomes distinguishable from zero for final eccentricities $e_f \gtrsim 0.05$, while single-harmonic sensitivity declines beyond $e_f \simeq 0.1$. In noise-free analyses, including enough harmonics to recover at least $50\%$ of the total SNR avoids this degradation, but adds statistical freedom when calibrating detection thresholds. We apply the framework to two astrophysical EMRI populations, showing how these competing effects interact with their eccentricity distributions under simplified assumptions. The balance among eccentricity-driven distinguishability, harmonic power loss, and the statistical cost of additional harmonics is an important consideration for future EMRI search developments.

Leveraging Lensing-driven Early Warnings: Joint Detectability of lensed Gravitational Waves and their LSST Optical Counterparts

Sourabh Magare, Anindya, Anupreeta More, Shasvath J. Kapadia

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing

2611.14343v1 · astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.CO

First submitted: 10 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 10 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

Gravitational lensing of gravitational waves (GWs) can be leveraged to provide early-warning times of $\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{hours})$ to $\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{days})$ prior to the mergers of binary neutron stars (BNSs) and neutron star-black holes (NSBHs). This capability could, in turn, enable electromagnetic (EM) observatories to capture transient emissions surrounding the time of the merger. In this work, we demonstrate the viability of jointly detecting gravitationally lensed BNS and NSBH mergers via GWs during the O5 observing run, alongside their associated kilonova counterparts using the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST). We simulate a population of lensed BNS and NSBH mergers detectable during O5 and utilize current benchmark forward models to generate the corresponding lensed kilonova light curves. We then assess the detectability of these light curves across LSST bands under various exposure limits. We find that among the detectable lensed mergers in O5, approximately 60\% of the BNS kilonova and 10\% of the NSBH kilonova will be observable within a single LSST exposure, with mostly quads dominating the detectable population. Furthermore, we define a detectability index ($\rm DI$), which determines the EM-detectability in optical of lensed GW-detectable BNS and NSBH mergers. We demonstrate the robustness and generalizability of the $\rm DI$ on our lensed BNS and NSBH population. Our work demonstrates that lensing-driven early warnings will be highly applicable in the near future, enabling the LSST to effectively follow up on BNS and NSBH triggers within GW localization areas during the O5 observing run.

Response of Circular Binaries to Monochromatic Gravitational Waves

Corey McInerney, Phil J. Sutton

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

The exchange in specific orbital energy between a test particle in a Keplerian binary orbit and a continuously incident linearized gravitational wave (GW) is considered. Analytical expressions for the rate of change of energy, semi-major axis and mean motion of the test particle are derived. Numerical solutions illustrate a resonance effect betwen the GWs frequency and the particle's initial orbital frequency. We distinguish between bounded short-period oscillations and resonant libration. The effect of the GWs angle of incidence with the particle's orbital plane is also considered. The out-state of scalar-induced gravitational waves

Yizhou Lu

Gravitational waves Resonance

2611.13462v1 · gr-qc, hep-th

First submitted: 10 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 10 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

Scalar-induced gravitational waves (SIGWs) are described by second-order tensor perturbations that contain free radiation together with a gauge-dependent response maintained by the scalar source. For a perfect fluid with constant equation of state $0<w<1$, we identify the free radiation with the out-state of the tensor perturbation, assembled from the light-cone residue of kernel functions for each pair of scalar modes. Its amplitudes follow from a classical LSZ-type reduction formula, in terms of the initial data and the light-cone-projected source. We prove a classical equivalence theorem: a redefinition of the tensor perturbation whose late-time shift has power-law envelopes and no tensor-frequency component leaves these amplitudes unchanged. Transformations between intrinsic gauges, whose slice-normal observers and spatial coordinates are fixed by the matter and geometry without an external clock or independent oscillatory dynamics, are redefinitions of this kind, except on resonance surfaces of zero measure. The out-state is therefore the same in all intrinsic gauges, including the standard gauges of cosmological perturbation theory. The late-time Newtonian kernel is then the out-state kernel, which justifies its use for the energy spectrum of the free radiation. In matter domination, the out-state instead separates the free radiation from the static tidal response.

Eccentricity greatly enhances the detectability of line-of-sight acceleration in gravitational wave signals

János Takátsy, Anna Puecher, Gonzalo Morras, Lorenz Zwick

Gravitational waves Environmental effects

2611.13197v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 09 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 09 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Environmental effects

Read the author-provided abstract

Compact object binaries formed dynamically are characterized by a tail of eccentric sources and by the potential presence of environmental perturbations. Here we show that the presence of eccentricity in a gravitational-wave (GW) signal strongly enhances the sensitivity to environmental effects, focusing in particular on line-of-sight acceleration (LSA). We demonstrate that this enhancement is recovered in Fisher-analysis forecasts and, crucially, persists in full Bayesian parameter estimation using state-of-the-art eccentric waveform models. The key insight is that the signal should be analyzed from the moment the first detectable eccentric harmonic enters the detector band, rather than from the customary entry of the $n=2$ harmonic: this allows to probe the early inspiral and thus a longer signal, over which the GW dephasing introduced by LSA becomes substantially larger. We show that with this approach constraints on LSA can increase by more than an order of magnitude between eccentricities at 20~Hz $e_{20}=0$ and $e_{20}\sim0.1$, for signals with sufficient signal-to-noise-ratio (SNR), while we predict a factor $5$ amplification even for low-SNR ones. Fully exploiting this effect necessitates the continued improvement of efficient eccentric waveform models and likelihood evaluations, enabling the efficient analysis of signals lasting thousands of seconds and containing a large number of harmonics.

Shaping black hole resonances II: Spectroscopic landscapes and interference in black hole ringdown

Alejandro Svyatkovskyy Kholyavka, Sayak Datta, Jose Antonio León Vega, Xisco Jiménez Forteza

Resonance Quasi-normal modes

2611.13075v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 09 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 09 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

We map analytically the excitation of Schwarzschild quasinormal modes (QNMs) $C_n$ by oscillatory Gaussian perturbations across the pulse width $σ$ and carrier frequency $ν$. The excitation coefficient of each mode is determined by the product of an intrinsic excitation factor and a source-dependent overlap evaluated at the complex QNM frequency. For oscillatory Gaussian perturbations, this overlap is the coherent sum of the positive- and negative-frequency branches of the source, with its amplitude factorizing exactly into a Gaussian spectral envelope and an interference factor. We observe that the excitation amplitude peaks at $ν=ω_n^{\rm Re}$ only for narrowband sources. For sufficiently broadband pulses, the maximum lies at $ν=0$, so efficient excitation does not necessarily require matching the carrier and the mode frequency. We observe that the interference between branches, modulates the amplitude only for nonzero $ν$, a feature generic to real oscillatory sources. The prominence of the interference-induced modulation is controlled by the ratio $ω_n^{\rm Re}/ω_n^{\rm Im}$. The phase separates into a mode-only, a width-only and a $ν$-dependent term, the last generated by the same interference, with $ν=0$ being its unique maximum at fixed width. The analytical predictions are validated against time-domain numerical simulations, showing a relative median error of $\sim 4\%$ for $|C_0|$. For $|C_1|$, the median error is $\sim 16\%$ when $σ\leq 5$, increasing to $\sim 36\%$ across the full parameter space. The median phase difference for $n=0$ is $\sim0.14 \,\rm rad $ and for $n=1$, $σ\leq5$ it is $0.34\,\rm rad$. Our results demonstrate that QNM excitation cannot be understood through frequency matching alone and may provide qualitative insights into more realistic astrophysical scenarios.

Chaotic motion of spinning particles in nonlocal gravity

Jianhui Lin, Xiangdong Zhang

EMRIs Resonance

2611.19191v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 14 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 14 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. EMRIs; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

We study spin-induced chaos around a static, spherically symmetric black hole in revised Deser--Woodard nonlocal gravity (NLG), using the conservative test-body approximation relevant to extreme-mass-ratio inspirals. The deformation parameter $ζ$ describes departures from Schwarzschild spacetime. Poincaré sections, rotation curves, and fast Lyapunov indicators (FLIs) reveal changes in resonant structures and a decreasing fraction of non-plunging trajectories classified as chaotic within the sampled family, from $37.5\%$ at $ζ=0$ to zero at $ζ=0.1$. The FLI classifications of selected trajectories are reflected in their approximate mass-quadrupole waveforms: neighboring chaotic and regular trajectories show a marked local spectral-flatness contrast, which weakens in backgrounds where both are classified as regular. To assess whether a transition from chaotic to regular motion caused by the background deformation helps distinguish NLG from GR, we compare NLG waveforms at $ζ=10^{-6}$ with GR waveforms generated at different initial radii. For a trajectory whose classification changes between the backgrounds, this adjustment substantially reduces the mismatch. The selected NLG waveforms from chaotic orbits retain larger minimum mismatches than those from regular orbits, although sensitivity to initial conditions may also hinder signal recovery. These results connect background-dependent orbital dynamics with radiation spectra, but do not establish that chaos offers an advantage for identifying the background geometry.

Cosmography with gravitational waves II: Padé approximants in an extended redshift regime

Jonathan Morais, Rodrigo Gonçalves, Jailson Alcaniz

Gravitational waves

2611.19006v1 · astro-ph.CO, gr-qc

First submitted: 13 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 13 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves

Read the author-provided abstract

We evaluate the capability of next-generation gravitational-wave detectors, including LISA, the Einstein Telescope, and DECIGO, to probe cosmographic parameters using electromagnetically bright standard sirens. Adopting Padé approximants, we extend the cosmographic analysis to substantially higher redshifts and constrain the parameters $H_0$, $q_0$, $j_0$, $s_0$, and $l_0$. We examine the constraints primarily as a function of the number of detections and, in a complementary analysis, across correlated SNR--redshift regimes. Compared with the low-redshift Taylor analysis, the Padé approach extends the usable redshift range, increases the accessible event population and enables a higher-order reconstruction of the expansion history.

Constraining deviations from the Teukolsky equation with GW250114

Sebastian H. Völkel, Nicola Franchini

Gravitational waves Quasi-normal modes Teukolsky

2607.26561v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 29 Jul 2026 · Last updated: 12 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Quasi-normal modes; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

The recent gravitational-wave detection GW250114 by the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) Collaboration provides unprecedented precision for testing general relativity (GR) through black hole ringdowns. In this study, we provide the first bounds on theory-agnostic deviations from the Teukolsky equation as described by the beyond-Teukolsky formalism. It directly connects deviations in the perturbation equations on the level of the effective potential in the Teukolsky equation with changes in the quasinormal mode (QNM) spectrum. We incorporate information on the final mass and spin from a full LVK inspiral-merger-ringdown analysis as parametrized priors in our analysis, reflecting theoretical uncertainties. Using publicly available LVK posterior information on agnostic damped sinusoid parameters, we then demonstrate how much beyond-Teukolsky potentials can be constrained. The high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) allows us to avoid the expensive full Bayesian analysis of all parameters and to work directly with a simplified likelihood for the fundamental QNM only. This strategy is promising for future events with even higher SNR and allows, in principle, for a quick and simple test of theories beyond GR without performing the full data analysis procedure. We report that current bounds on deviation parameters are in agreement with the Teukolsky equation.

Popcorn EMRIs: Transient Gravitational Wave Signals and Their Analysis in Schwartz Space

Pau Amaro Seoane, Kostas Tzanavaris

Gravitational waves EMRIs Collaborator: Pau Amaro-Seoane

2512.04167v3 · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO

First submitted: 03 Dec 2025 · Last updated: 12 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

This study investigates extreme-mass ratio inspirals (EMRIs) with orbital periods exceeding the observation windows of mHz gravitational wave detectors. During their early, highly eccentric stages, these systems produce periodic gravitational wave eruptions at pericentre, separated by long quiescent periods. We define these systems as ``popcorn EMRIs''--bound, coherent binaries in a deterministic evolutionary phase. This distinguishes them from stochastic, non-coherent bursts associated with parabolic fly-bys. Using a steady-state model based on the phase-space continuity equation, we estimate the population within a Milky Way-like galaxy. Because these estimates depend on sensitive astrophysical variables like stellar relaxation and density profiles, our figures are illustrative rather than absolute. For a one-year observation baseline, we estimate a steady-state population of 200 to 2,000 popcorn EMRIs, significantly outnumbering the 10 to 88 continuously observable EMRIs. This yields an expected rate of 5 to 44 detectable eruptions per year. Despite high signal-to-noise ratios (exceeding 700 for Galactic Centre events), the low duty cycle confirms their transient nature. Standard data processing techniques, designed for continuous signals, often employ windowing that distorts eruption morphology and artificially reduces the signal-to-noise ratio. Read the author-provided abstract

The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will detect gravitational waves (GWs) from dozens of extreme- and intermediate-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs/IMRIs). These sources will stay in-band for months to years, offering extraordinary scientific potential. However, their fully phase-coherent analysis standard in current pipelines imposes stringent waveform accuracy requirements; failing to model the signal over such long durations can result in significant systematic biases. To address this, we formulate a robust Bayesian inference framework that segments the data into blocks, infers each block coherently, and recombines the results using loosely-coherent phase information. By restricting full phase-tracking to much shorter block durations, this approach prevents significant accumulation of phase errors. We implement this methodology in \textsc{SPLIT} (Segmented Posteriors for Long-Inspiral Templates), a GPU-accelerated Python package. Applying \textsc{SPLIT} to an environment-rich injection, we demonstrate that while a fully-coherent vacuum-GR analysis incurs a maximum 1D systematic bias of $\approx 4.8σ$ from the truth, the shorter integration window of our loosely-coherent approach restricts such biases to $\lesssim 0.5σ$. Overall, despite a fractional loss of optimal signal-to-noise ratio, the substantial improvement in parameter accuracy offered by our method presents a highly advantageous trade-off for LISA and other future GW detectors.

We develop a perturbative framework for a black hole embedded in a generic, possibly anisotropic, matter environment under spherical symmetry. We present an implementation of an HLLD approximate Riemann solver for the AthenaK astrophysics code with support for full general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics via a tetrad frame transformation. We investigate the gravitational-wave signatures of equatorial reflection symmetry breaking in extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) around a parity-violating conformal Kerr black hole. For the circular-orbit branch connected to equatorial Kerr orbits, we derive the dynamics through second order in the dimensionless deformation parameter $\varepsilon$ and construct adiabatic inspirals and gravitational-wave signals using a mass-quadrupole prescription. Equatorial reflection maps $\varepsilon$ to $-\varepsilon$, making the polar displacement odd and the orbital frequency and energy even in the deformation. A single-parameter Fisher analysis also indicates improved local sensitivity to $\varepsilon$ at high spin. These results connect the reflection properties of the circular branch to accumulated source-phase effects and complement tests of propagation birefringence.

Impact on Inferred Neutron Star Equation of State due to Nonlinear Hydrodynamics, Background Spin, and Relativity

Joseph Bretz, Hang Yu

2602.04951v2 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 04 Feb 2026 · Last updated: 10 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance; Tidal resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

Tidal interaction is a unique, detectable signature in gravitational wave signals from inspiraling binary neutron stars (BNSs), which can be used to constrain the neutron star (NS) equation of state (EoS). The tidal interaction is resonantly amplified as the orbital frequency approaches the NS fundamental mode (f-mode) frequency. It has been shown that the exclusion of tidal resonance in parameter estimation leads to a significant bias in the inferred NS tidal deformability and hence the NS EoS [Pratten et al. PRL 129, 081102 (2022)]. The strength and location of tidal resonance depend sensitively on the f-mode frequency, which is typically modeled using its universal relation with the tidal deformability that is derived for an isolated, non-spinning NS assuming only linear fluid perturbations. In a BNS inspiral, the f-mode frequency can be corrected by at least three known effects: nonlinear hydrodynamics, background spin, and relativity. We use Hamiltonian Monte Carlo simulations to estimate the systematic bias on tidal deformability when each frequency correction is ignored. Our study considers both loud, individual events and the stacking of a population of detections. Both scenarios are expected when the next-generation detectors are available with a sensitivity level increased by about an order of magnitude.
