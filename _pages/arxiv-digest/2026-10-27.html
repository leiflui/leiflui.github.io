Neutron Star--Primordial Black Hole Binaries: Dynamical Tides, Resonance Locking, and Gravitational Waves

Isaac Legred, Nicolás Yunes

Gravitational waves Resonance Collaborator: Nicholas Yunes

2610.33314v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 23 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 23 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

The orbital evolution of a neutron star and a much lower-mass primordial black hole can differ strikingly from that of typical compact binaries. Dynamical tides, analogous to those in the Solar System's giant planet-moon systems, can accelerate inspiral or induce outspiral, depending on the orbital configuration. We show how gravitational-wave emission mediates this evolution when neutron star modes remain locked in resonance with the orbit. These effects may leave observable signatures in pulsar timing or gravitational waves.

Gravitational waves from inclined orbits around Kerr black holes

Yi-Han Huang, Sen Guo, Yu Liang, Lin Wen, Kai Lin

Gravitational waves EMRIs Resonance

2610.31697v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 21 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 21 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

Extreme mass ratio inspirals on generic inclined orbits provide a critical dynamical probe of strong field gravity, as they uniquely break the severe parameter degeneracies inherent to planar equatorial trajectories. To isolate the exact rotational signatures, we extend the periodic-orbit framework to three-dimensional inclined trajectories in the Kerr spacetime, specifically focusing on the $r$--$θ$ orbital resonances. By mapping the geodesic motion into a co-precessing spatial framework parameterized by Mino time, we compute the emitted quadrupole waveforms and evolve the local dynamics using circularity-preserving radiation reaction. It is demonstrated that frame-dragging induces a transient dynamical tilt at periastron, which directly splits the emitted radiation into an exact five-comb spectral architecture. When this resonant structure is resolved over a single coherence time, the statistical error on the dimensionless spin is reduced to $σ_a \approx 10^{-2}$, improving upon the equatorial baseline by up to two orders of magnitude. Because any bound orbit in a static, spherically symmetric spacetime generates a maximum of three frequency combs, the detection of this five-comb hierarchy establishes a rigorous, model-independent null test to strictly exclude spherical geometries.

Lasting mEMRIs: Signatures of Multiple Black Hole Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals from AGN discs

Jordan W. N. Moncrieff, Evgeni Grishin, Fiona H. Panther, Alessandro A. Trani

Gravitational waves EMRIs Resonance

2610.29376v1 · astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.GA, gr-qc

First submitted: 21 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 21 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

Encounters of stellar-mass black holes (sBH) in active galactic nuclei discs naturally form mean-motion resonances of sBH pairs. These mean-motion resonances can be broken close to the central supermassive black hole when gravitational-wave emission dominates over gas torques. We derive a simple condition that depends only on the sBH mass ratio and the resonance index for which a pair of sBH in a mean-motion resonance will remain intact and can be detectable as a multiple Extreme-Mass-Ratio-Inspirals (mEMRIs) in the LISA band. We present for the first time the gravitational-wave strain signatures of a pair of co-migrating sBH in mean-motion resonance, including additional gravitational-wave harmonics associated with orbital conjunctions of the individual EMRIs. The frequency evolution and the associated chirp mass is degenerate with isolated EMRIs of different masses. However, the mEMRI mass ratio is quantised by the resonance order and is larger than the true mass ratio. The degeneracy can be broken if the true sBH mass ratio can be inferred from individual strain amplitudes. We argue that mEMRIs should be common if disc driven EMRIs are to be detected by LISA, with $\sim 10$ mEMRIs per year in the local universe ($z \lesssim 0.2$), highlighting the need to model mEMRI systems in future LISA data analysis.

Gravitational-Wave Signatures and Periodic Orbits of Charged Particles Around a Five-Dimensional Rotating Black Hole: Comparison with the Kerr Black Hole

Bahromjon Shokirov, Tursunali Xamidov, Chen Zhou, Sanjar Shaymatov, Chengxun Yuan

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2610.35386v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 26 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 26 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

We study periodic orbits of charged particles in the equatorial plane of a five-dimensional ($D=5$) rotating Myers--Perry black hole (BH) immersed in a uniform magnetic field, and the gravitational waves emitted by these orbits in an extreme mass-ratio inspiral. All results are compared with the Kerr BH in the uniform magnetic field. We find that a charged particle is much more weakly bound to the $D=5$ dimensional BH, i.e. its energy at the innermost stable circular orbit is close to unity, $0.95$--$0.99$, compared with $0.78$--$0.94$ for the Kerr BH, and the depth of the potential well is several times smaller. As a result, the periodic orbits of the $D=5$ dimensional BH lie much closer to the separatrix. The magnetic field can make the azimuthal frequency of the orbit smaller than the radial one and produces loops in the orbits far from the BH. Using the numerical kludge method, we compute the gravitational waveforms for a compact object of $10M_\odot$ orbiting a BH of $10^6M_\odot$. At the same magnetic field, the $D=5$ dimensional signal has a longer period, lower frequencies, and an amplitude smaller by about $18\%$. We show that for the magnetic parameter $βM=0.02$ the strongest spectral line is at $7.9$\,mHz, compared with $9.9$\,mHz for the Kerr BH. An estimate of the radiation reaction shows that the periodic orbits survive from less than an hour to a few days in the presence of an external magnetic field. Over these lifetimes, the signal-to-noise ratio for LISA is $7$--$31$ for the rotating BH in $D=5$ dimensions, compared to the Kerr BH where it is about $15$--$54$.

Quasinormal modes of thick branes in $f(R,T)$ gravity

Yu-Peng E, Hai-Long Jia, Chun-Chun Zhu

Resonance Quasi-normal modes

2610.35096v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 26 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 26 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

We study tensor perturbations and quasinormal modes (QNMs) of thick branes in $f(R,T)=R+αT$ gravity. Starting from the background thick brane solutions, we derive the Schrödinger-like equation for gravitational perturbations and construct the corresponding effective potential. The effective potential exhibits a double-barrier structure whose properties depend on the model parameters. We analyze transmission spectra and find good agreement between resonance peaks and the real parts of the quasinormal frequencies. The parameters $b$ and $α$ mainly control the width and height of the potential barriers, respectively. QNMs are extracted using both time-domain evolution and direct integration methods, showing consistent results for low-lying modes. Higher overtones become increasingly difficult to extract due to stronger damping and numerical contamination. Our results indicate that $f(R,T)$ corrections significantly affect both the effective potential and the quasinormal spectrum, providing a possible imprint of modified gravity in braneworld perturbations.

Gravitational Waves from Ringing Dyonic Black Holes and Estimation of Parameters in Einstein-Bumblebee-Maxwell Gravity

Sikha Gogoi, Umananda Dev Goswami

Gravitational waves Quasi-normal modes

2610.33569v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 24 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 24 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

In this work, we investigate the quasinormal mode (QNM) frequencies of dyonic Reissner-Nordström (RN)-like black holes associated with scalar and gravitational perturbations in the framework of Einstein-bumblebee-Maxwell gravity theory. The QNMs are calculated using the 6th-order Padé averaged WKB approximation method. We analyse the dependence of oscillation frequencies and damping rate of the BHs for the free model parameters. To further verify the QNM spectrum, we study the time evolution of both the scalar and gravitational perturbations around the BHs and extract the dominant QNM from the resulting ringdown signal. Moreover, we try to estimate the BH parameters using Fisher analysis method for the future gravitational wave detector named Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA). We found that for supermassive BHs with mass around $10^6$ -- $10^8 M_\odot$ and at lower redshifts, the signal-to-noise ratio for the detection of a QNM signal by the LISA is high. Further, for a representative source at a luminosity distance of $3$ Gpc, the BH mass at the detector reference frame and the Lorentz violating parameter are well constrained for supermassive BHs with mass $\ge 10^6.$

Gravitational Wave Diffractive Images Reveal the Structure of Astrophysical Objects

Han Gil Choi, Sunghoon Jung, Volodymyr Takhistov

Gravitational waves Environmental effects

2610.31441v1 · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, gr-qc, hep-ph

First submitted: 22 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 22 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Environmental effects

Read the author-provided abstract

Scattering patterns encode the structure of extended objects, as demonstrated by charged particle scattering on atomic nuclei. Gravitational wave (GW) diffraction offers an analogous probe of otherwise invisible mass distributions. At linear order in the lens potential we derive expressions relating the central diffractive image contribution to a gravitationally lensed GW waveform to the Fourier transform of the projected lens density. For finite mass lenses this contribution takes the form of a point mass Born amplitude multiplied by a normalized mass form factor, analogous to that in nuclear scattering. As the frequency of GWs from a merging compact binary source evolves the signal samples this form factor across spatial scales, imprinting lens structure through interference with the primary image. Using full numerical wave optics calculations we quantify the observational reach for extended dark matter objects, primordial black holes halo-dressed by dark matter halos and superradiance clouds of ultralight bosons around black holes. For the considered source populations, five year observations with next generation detectors can distinguish extended lenses from point masses, probing dark matter fractions down to $\mathcal{O}(10^{-5})$, and halo-dressed primordial black holes from matched bare black holes, probing primordial black hole dark matter fractions down to $\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})$. For sufficiently close source-lens configurations, scalar superradiance clouds can produce distinguishable diffraction signatures for boson masses of approximately $10^{-17}-10^{-12}~\mathrm{eV}$. These results provide a framework for characterizing extended dark matter and compact object astrophysical environments.

A superconducting LC resonator in a multi-tesla magnetic field for tabletop kHz gravitational-wave detection

D. Ahn, C. Braggio, G. Carugno, D. D'Agostino, R. Di Vora, P. Falferi, A. Ortolan, G. Ruoso, M. L. Ruggiero

Gravitational waves Resonance

2610.31430v1 · hep-ex, gr-qc

First submitted: 22 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 22 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

We present a concept for a superconducting inductance--capacitance (LC) resonator immersed in a magnetic field for gravitational-wave detection in the kHz band. In the long-wavelength limit, a gravitational wave in a static magnetic field induces an oscillating magnetic-field perturbation proportional to the static magnetic field applied to the resonator and to the gravitational-wave strain. We show that a superconducting pickup circuit can convert this perturbation into a flux signal read out by a superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID)-based or quantum-limited amplifier chain. Inspired by resonant-mass detectors, we develop a three-loop coupled-circuit model and derive the equivalent strain-noise spectrum, including thermal noise, SQUID backaction, and measurement imprecision. Prototype measurements demonstrate a quality factor of $Q\simeq 5\times 10^5$, while $Q=10^7$ is considered as a future development target. Under the assumed flux coupling and an idealized quantum-limited SQUID noise model, optimized circuits yield projected strain sensitivities of order $10^{-21}\,\mathrm{Hz}^{-1/2}$ over $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$ bands in the $1$--$20\,\mathrm{kHz}$ range. Increasing the active high-field volume and improving quantum-limited readout could enable further sensitivity gains. This compact, tunable detector concept could complement interferometric gravitational-wave searches and motivate investigations of high-frequency astrophysical sources.

Exceptional points and hysteresis in Kerr-Newman quasi-normal modes

Mario Misas Arcos, Alberto T. Pérez, Carlos F. Sopuerta

Quasi-normal modes Teukolsky

2610.30049v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, hep-th

First submitted: 21 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 21 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

Exceptional points (EPs) and eigenstate hysteresis, previously found in Kerr only with an auxiliary scalar field, arise intrinsically in the quasinormal-mode spectrum of Kerr-Newman black holes. Using a hyperboloidal formulation of the coupled gravito-electromagnetic Teukolsky equations and a Chebyshev pseudospectral method, we compute the $\ell=m=2$ spectrum and locate four EPs at up to $99.974\%$ of extremality, one from a new repulsion. The gaps close as a square root branch point, and quasistatic loops around one or more EPs exchange the coalescing modes, confirming hysteresis.

Untangling Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals and Massive Black Hole Binaries in LISA Data

Rishav Agrawal, Clémence Allietta, Ollie Burke, Alvin J. K. Chua

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2610.27642v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.IM

First submitted: 20 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 20 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will observe sources in the millihertz regime, such as Galactic Binaries (GBs), Massive Black Hole Binaries (MBHBs), and Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals (EMRIs), so its data stream will be a superposition of an unknown number of gravitational-wave sources. We consider the joint inference of various representative combinations of MBHB and EMRI signals in LISA data (with assumed instrumental noise and stochastic GB foreground). Our Bayesian analyses confirm the expectation of weak inter-source coupling between EMRIs, and between EMRIs and MBHBs, despite strong intra-source parameter correlations. Weak EMRI-MBHB coupling enables a hierarchically sequential approach to EMRI and MBHB inference, rather than fully joint inference. We further show that imperfect MBHB subtraction, arising from missing modes or overlapping MBHBs, has only a weak impact on EMRI inference. For multiple MBHBs, we mitigate inference errors through a joint MBHB fit. We demonstrate this strategy on simulated LISA data containing noise, three MBHBs, and three EMRIs, jointly fitting and subtracting the MBHBs before sequentially inferring the EMRIs - ultimately observing little to no bias on all recovered astrophysical parameters. The notions described throughout our work are highly relevant to informing optimal strategies for the global-fit-based pipelines that are currently being developed for LISA.

Curvature Reconstruction and Tensor Polarizations in Isospectrality-Breaking Black-Hole Perturbations

Pratik Wagle, Dongjun Li, Yanbei Chen, Nicolas Yunes

Gravitational waves Black-hole perturbation theory Collaborator: Nicholas Yunes Collaborator: Dongjun Li Notable author: Yanbei Chen

2610.27116v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 19 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 19 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Black-hole perturbation theory

Read the author-provided abstract

Testing general relativity with the ringdown phase of gravitational waves requires connecting modifications of black-hole perturbation dynamics to observable signatures in detector strain. In this work, we study the relation between curvature perturbations, metric master variables, and detector tensor polarizations for nonrotating black holes in dynamical Chern-Simons gravity. In this theory the axial gravitational perturbation couples to a dynamical scalar field, while the polar sector remains GR-like at the order considered. We first construct a map for the perturbed Weyl scalar $δΨ_0$, expressing its even-parity part in terms of the ZM master function and its odd-parity part in terms of the RW master function together with the scalar radial perturbation. We also derive the leading asymptotic tensor polarizations $h_+$ and $h_\times$ directly from the metric perturbations. The resulting strain is reconstructed from the radiative gravitational master functions: the ZM function in the even sector and the Cunningham-Price-Moncrief-normalized odd function in the axial sector for dynamical Chern-Simons gravity. No independent local algebraic term proportional to the scalar radial function appears in the leading tensor strain. These results separate curvature-level scalar dependence from tensor-channel scalar-frequency responses and provide the formal reconstruction basis for companion work on parity-split beating and effective ringdown frequencies.

The long-term evolution of a black hole accretion torus: a comparison between GRMHD simulations and relativistic thin disk theory

Andrew Mummery, Minghao Guo, Eliot Quataert, James M. Stone

GRMHD

2610.35648v1 · astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 26 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 26 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. GRMHD

Read the author-provided abstract

In this paper we analyse the long-term evolution of a black hole accretion torus using novel GRMHD simulations which, for the first time, have been run out to extremely large evolutionary times $t_{\rm max} = 10^7 GM_\bullet/c^3$. This allows for detailed comparisons between the gross evolution of this GRMHD simulation and existing "spreading disk" analytical theories. We show that the long-term features of this evolution are well described by General Relativistic Thin Disk Theory (GRTDT), which reproduces various aspects of the GRMHD evolution to within a factor $\sim 2$, despite the disk in our simulation not being remotely thin, showing dynamically dominant (MAD) magnetic fields, and launching a powerful wind. Thin disk theory functions as an accurate and cheap effective theory for the evolution in radius and time of the bulk of the disk material, and for the time dependence of the horizon accretion rate. We argue that this effectiveness originates from the global conservation laws of mass and angular momentum, together with a turbulent stress which we find to be local and independent of density, lending support to the ad-hoc local-stress-tensor phenomenology employed in analytical models. We also find that the turbulent accretion rate variability in the inner disk is log-normally distributed, consistent with a key assumption in the modeling of observed variability.

Detecting Tidal Resonances in Binary Neutron Stars

Fabian Gittins, Harsh Narola, Thibeau Wouters, Peter T. H. Pang, Tanja Hinderer, Chris Van Den Broeck

Gravitational waves Resonance Tidal resonance

2606.06376v2 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 04 Jun 2026 · Last updated: 20 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance; Tidal resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

As a binary neutron star inspirals due to the emission of gravitational waves, the rising tidal frequency resonantly excites vibrational modes. These oscillations are seismological probes of the rich stellar interior, yet it remains to be established whether gravitational-wave interferometers can measure them. Here, we present the first fully Bayesian study of the capability of the Einstein Telescope to detect tidal resonances. We simulate one year of observations and analyse the 200 loudest signals. We find that the Einstein Telescope can identify resonant modes and is sensitive to gravitational-wave phase shifts as small as $ΔΦ\approx 0.03$ for favourable events. We further show that neglecting resonances can bias the inferred tidal deformabilities. These results establish tidal resonances as a measurable route for asteroseismology with future detectors.

Discrete symmetries of modified Teukolsky equations

Ciro De Simone

Quasi-normal modes Black-hole perturbation theory Teukolsky

2607.27053v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 29 Jul 2026 · Last updated: 25 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Black-hole perturbation theory; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

The Teukolsky equation possesses discrete symmetries that constrain the properties of black hole perturbations and their quasinormal mode spectrum. In this study, we explore how a class of modifications of the Teukolsky potential can alter the symmetry structure of the equation and break the m = 0 degeneracy of quasinormal modes. We prove this result in frequency domain using the master Teukolsky equation and in time domain via (2+1)-dimensional simulations. We also show that the discrete symmetries can be leveraged for a more efficient characterization of the m = 0 quasinormal modes from time-domain evolutions. As a theory-specific application, we consider the case of higher-derivative theories of gravity, highlighting that time-domain implementations of frequency-domain potentials can give rise to additional non-physical branches of modes.

Polarization-Dependent Photon Propagation, Quasinormal Modes, and Gravitational Lensing in Higher-Curvature Effective Theories

Takamasa Kanai

Gravitational lensing Quasi-normal modes

2606.19773v3 · gr-qc, hep-th

First submitted: 18 Jun 2026 · Last updated: 20 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational lensing; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

We investigate the impact of higher-curvature corrections on photon propagation within an effective field theory framework and their observational consequences in strong gravitational fields. We consider polarization-dependent modifications to photon trajectories in static and spherically symmetric spacetimes, focusing on Schwarzschild and Reissner--Nordström black hole backgrounds. Using the geometrical optics approximation, we derive the effective metrics governing photon propagation and study the resulting polarization-dependent shifts of the photon sphere. We compute the corresponding quasinormal modes in the eikonal limit and analyze their polarization dependence. We further investigate gravitational lensing, focusing on polarization-dependent corrections to the deflection angle in both weak- and strong-field regimes. In the strong-deflection regime, we find that even perturbatively small EFT corrections modify the coefficient of the logarithmically divergent part of the deflection angle, resulting in a potentially observable difference from the uncorrected case. This suggests that strong gravitational lensing may provide a sensitive probe of small higher-curvature corrections. While extracting EFT information directly from QNM frequencies is more subtle, QNMs may provide complementary information to gravitational lensing in future studies. Our results establish a framework for probing higher-curvature effects through polarization-dependent strong-field observables.

We present a scheme and implementation code for time-domain integration of the Teukolsky equation in 1+1 dimensions with a point-particle source, based on comoving, spatially compactified hyperboloidal coordinates. We demonstrate that the scheme evades the problem of nonphysical growing modes that plague some numerical evolution schemes without compactification. Our use of comoving coordinates greatly simplifies the application of jump conditions on the particle's worldline. We develop our method and test its performance for a scalar field on a Schwarzschild background, first for a circular geodesic orbit source and then for a scattering geodesic orbit. Our method paves the way to calculations of the full gravitational self-force in extreme-mass-ratio scattering.

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing Notable author: João S. Santos

First submitted: 13 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 26 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

Compact binaries evolving in the vicinity of massive black holes may be commonplace in the Universe, and their gravitational waves carry signatures of their environment. We study the strong lensing of these signals by the massive companion, a configuration that violates the standard assumptions of the lensing literature: geometric optics and thin lenses. Using a perturbative framework, we compute the full wave optics amplification factors for face-on and edge-on binaries, including spinning lenses. In the edge-on configuration, peak amplification does not occur at perfect source--lens--observer alignment, even for a non-spinning lens. This is a consequence of the anisotropic, coherent quadrupolar emission of the stellar-mass binary breaking the axisymmetry of the system. Restricting to the equatorial plane near the optical axis, we then find that the frequency dependence of the amplification factor is universal across every configuration studied, persisting deep into the wave optics regime. Comparing against a strong-field ray tracing calculation, we find agreement at the $\sim10\%$ level for a face-on binary lensed by a Schwarzschild black hole. In the less symmetric edge-on case only the angular peak shift and fringe spacing is reproduced to this accuracy. For a Kerr lens, the face-on amplification factor is described by its Schwarzschild counterpart to the same $\sim 10 \%$ accuracy once the effect of frame dragging is corrected for. A point mass lens, by contrast, fails to reproduce the features of the system, which may have strong implications for forecasts that adopt it for lensing by massive black holes in the strong field.

Targeting black holes from metal-poor progenitors with next-generation gravitational-wave detectors

Federico Leto di Priolo, Martyna Chruślińska, Davide Gerosa

Gravitational waves Environmental effects

2606.03776v2 · astro-ph.HE, gr-qc

First submitted: 02 Jun 2026 · Last updated: 24 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Environmental effects

Read the author-provided abstract

Next-generation gravitational-wave detectors such as the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer will be able to detect binary black-hole mergers out to the cosmic dawn. Mergers observed in the local Universe represent a mixture of systems formed across the entire cosmic history, spanning a wide range of astrophysical environments. Iron-group elements govern metallicity effects on stellar evolution, making metallicity a key tracer that leaves a strong imprint on the black-hole population. We introduce the concept of a ``target'' redshift, $z_t$, defined as the epoch at which more than $90\%$ of stars form with metallicity $Z < 0.1\,Z_\odot$. This provides a straightforward way to isolate mergers originating from metal-poor environments. The determination of $z_t$ relies on the reconstruction of the cosmic star formation rate density as a function of iron abundance. This reconstruction is not unique, as it depends on the combination of different empirical scaling relations. Consequently, $z_t$ spans a broad range, from $z_t \sim 4$ to $z_t > 10$, depending on the adopted model variation. We present a statistical framework that enables rapid tests of astrophysical predictions against forecasted observations from next-generation \acrlong{gw} detectors. By quantifying variations in the binary black-hole merger-rate density between the target redshift and the local Universe, our approach maps evolutionary trends across parameter space and estimates the detection statistics required to distinguish genuine astrophysical variations from statistical fluctuations.

Hankel low-rank matrix approximation for gravitational-wave data analysis

Nicholas Geissler, Vladimir Strokov, Christian Kümmerle, Sergey Kushnarev, Emanuele Berti

Gravitational waves Quasi-normal modes

2603.15740v2 · astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM, gr-qc

First submitted: 16 Mar 2026 · Last updated: 21 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

Next-generation gravitational-wave (GW) detectors, such as the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA), will observe vast numbers of overlapping signals. Disentangling these signals from instrumental noise and from one another constitutes a significant data analysis challenge. We explore a denoising technique based on embedding time series into Hankel matrices: a superposition of $n$ (damped) sinusoids corresponds to a matrix of rank $2n$. Thus, the problem of signal extraction is reduced to a structured low-rank approximation problem. Using synthetic data tailored to GW applications, we benchmark three Hankel-based algorithms: ESPRIT, Cadzow iterations, and iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS). Our test scenarios include isolated and multi-component monochromatic signals, the resolution of sources with closely spaced frequencies, and the recovery of black hole quasinormal modes (QNM). All three algorithms achieve near-optimal performance consistent with Fisher matrix bounds, evidenced by an inverse-square scaling of the mismatch with the signal-to-noise ratio. Furthermore, a proof-of-concept application to numerical relativity waveforms validates the ability of these algorithms to extract QNM frequencies from ringdown signals. Hankel low-rank approximation therefore offers a transparent, computationally efficient avenue for preprocessing GW time series.
