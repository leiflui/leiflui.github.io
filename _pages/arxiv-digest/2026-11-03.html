Influence of $Δ$ resonances in hyperonic neutron stars on gravitational-wave inspiral signatures

Athira S., Vivek Baruah Thapa, Monika Sinha

Gravitational waves Resonance

2610.40441v1 · astro-ph.HE, hep-ph

First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

The composition of dense matter in neutron star interiors remains uncertain, with the possible appearance of the non-strange $Δ$ resonances providing an important source of theoretical uncertainty. In this work, we investigate the gravitational wave signatures of hyperonic neutron stars containing $Δ$ baryons within a density-dependent relativistic mean-field framework based on the DDME2 parametrization. Within this framework, the baryons interact among themselves via mesons. For $Δ$ baryons, the interaction is uncertain to date. We investigate how variations of the scalar and vector $Δ-$meson couplings, subject to \(x_{σΔ}-x_{ωΔ}=0.2\), modify the tidal properties and inspiral gravitational-wave signals. We find that the allowed variation in the $Δ-σ$ coupling leads to substantial changes in the tidal properties of the neutron stars, which in turn leave distinct imprints on the gravitational-wave waveforms, particularly in their frequency evolution, accumulated phase, and strain amplitude. These waveform differences arise through changes in tidal deformability and are not unique signatures of $Δ$ resonances. Future gravitational-wave observations may therefore constrain the $Δ-$meson couplings within the adopted dense-matter framework, subject to degeneracies with alternative EOS models.

Gravitational lensing of gravitational waves by a Kerr black hole

Jian-hua He

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing

2610.39181v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

We simulate the gravitational lensing of gravitational waves (GWs) by a Kerr black hole using a $(1+1)$-dimensional time-domain mode-sum approach. To enable efficient explicit time evolution, we implement a localized hyperboloidal foliation combined with a static field redefinition, ensuring bounded characteristic speeds across an extended, uniform tortoise-coordinate grid. Incident GW initial data are constructed by projecting a plane-wave Weyl tensor onto the Kinnersley tetrad. Through local spin-dyad rotations and Wigner $D$-matrix transformations, our framework accommodates both idealized wave packets and realistic binary coalescence signals at arbitrary incidence and polarization angles. To accurately resolve the lensed waveforms, we evolve over $68,000$ coupled $(j,m)$ modes. Our results reveal that a Kerr black hole acts as a diffraction-limited thick lens, focusing GWs into a forward beam. For idealized wave packets, the primary prompt pulse exhibits a spin-independent transverse Gaussian profile, whereas the subsequent lensing coda is sensitive to the black hole's spin. For binary coalescence signals, strong-field lensing substantially modifies the post-merger ringdown. However, the lensed inspiral and merger phases in Kerr and Schwarzschild backgrounds are virtually identical, yielding waveform mismatches below $2.2\times10^{-5}$ across various inclinations and polarizations. These findings demonstrate that non-spinning Schwarzschild models provide an accurate and computationally economical surrogate to search for strong-field lensing signatures with current ground-based detectors.

Spectral instability of charged scalar quasinormal modes and superradiance: Stabilizing the unstable

Kyriakos Destounis

Resonance Quasi-normal modes

2611.03070v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, hep-ph, hep-th

First submitted: 02 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 02 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

The spectral instability of black holes has become a central topic of the black-hole spectroscopy program. Small changes to the black-hole scattering potential are known to produce disproportionately large migrations of its quasinormal modes in the complex plane, but it is unclear whether this sensitivity carries over to energy extraction through superradiance. We study this relation for massless charged scalar fields on Reissner--Nordström and Reissner--Nordström--de Sitter backgrounds. A localized positive potential bump, small in mass-scaled height, is displaced outward from the light ring while the original radiative boundary conditions are preserved. In the asymptotically flat case, a quasinormal branch enters the superradiant frequency regime while remaining damped. By searching for competing modes, we show that the least-damped positive-frequency mode also enters this regime, after a series of mode overtakings driven by quasinormal-mode migration. The real-frequency amplification, nevertheless, is only weakly affected and develops no pronounced narrow resonance. In de Sitter black holes, the bump can move an initially growing monopole mode into the damped half of the complex plane, stabilizing selected configurations that are initially modally unstable. After stabilization, the peak superradiant amplification can remain above both its unperturbed value and a gain of $100\%$; it falls below $100\%$ only for more distant bump locations. A prescribed positive perturbation can thus suppress modal growth without changing the superradiant frequency bounds or immediately suppressing the scattering peak.

The Effective Kerr-Compton Tree Amplitude from Black Hole Perturbation Theory

N. Emil J. Bjerrum-Bohr, Gang Chen, Konstantinos Papadimos, Nabha Shah, Chenliang Su

Gravitational waves Black-hole perturbation theory

2610.40101v1 · hep-th, gr-qc

First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Black-hole perturbation theory

Read the author-provided abstract

We develop a new method to construct the tree-level Compton amplitude for gravitational-wave scattering off a Kerr black hole in post-Minkowskian effective field theory. Building on previous computations that required physical consistency of the amplitude, the method uses wave scattering functions from black hole perturbation theory to uniquely determine additional contact terms. We illustrate the iterative procedure by deriving explicit expressions up to sixth order in spin, including previously unknown sectors at the fifth and sixth orders in spin. The amplitude features complex transcendental structures and a leading dissipative contact term at sixth order in spin. We verify our result against known results in the literature and reproduce the one-loop tail result from black hole perturbation theory.

`Adding colour'' to the gravitational-wave sky by probing unexplored frequency bands has the potential to unveil new and unexpected phenomena in the Universe. The microhertz band, between pulsar timing arrays and LISA, is a particularly attractive target: it should host the extension of the signal recently found by pulsar timing arrays (if it is generated by a family of sources of gravitational waves), and it may harbour signals from the early Universe, primordial black holes or dark matter. RAKI: High-Order Alternative Finite Differences for General Relativistic Magnetohydrodynamics

Rahime Matur, Beyhan Karakas, Ian Hawke, Nils Andersson

Gravitational waves GRMHD

2610.39792v1 · astro-ph.HE, gr-qc, hep-ph, physics.comp-ph

First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; GRMHD

Read the author-provided abstract

Binary neutron star (BNS) and black hole--neutron star mergers are among the most important multimessenger sources. Observations of these events could provide valuable insights into several unresolved phenomena, ranging from the efficiency of magnetic field amplification during and after mergers to the formation of magnetars and jets. Understanding these phenomena requires accurate modelling of magnetic field evolution, which remains a challenge for state-of-the-art general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD) simulations. In this work, we present \texttt{RAKI} (Relativistic Astrophysics Code Implementation of the Alternative Finite Difference Approach for GRMHD), the first GRMHD code to simulate BNS mergers using high-order alternative finite-difference methods with adaptive-order WENO (AFD-WENO-AO) reconstruction and a fully non-staggered, node-centred vector-potential formulation. The code achieves up to ninth-order convergence in smooth test problems while accurately capturing discontinuities. In magnetised Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff (TOV) star tests, \texttt{RAKI} retains $99.2$ per cent of the initial maximum magnetic field strength over ${\sim}2$ ms, exhibiting an order of magnitude less magnetic field loss than a reference GRMHD code at the same resolution. In BNS merger simulations, fifth-order AFD-WENO-AO reconstruction yields approximately third-order convergence of the gravitational-wave phase in both magnetised and unmagnetised models. The discrete no-monopole constraint is preserved to machine precision without staggered grids, even during BNS mergers with moving refinement regions. These results establish \texttt{RAKI} as a high-order, non-staggered GRMHD framework for accurate modelling of magnetic field evolution in neutron star mergers and its implications for multimessenger astrophysics.

Quasinormal Mode Excitation in the Horizon Shear of Near-Extremal Kerr Black Holes

Brennen Black

EMRIs Quasi-normal modes

2610.39729v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. EMRIs; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

The horizon shear of a near-extremal Kerr black hole perturbed by an eccentric extreme mass ratio inspiral shows low amplitude, high frequency oscillations (''wiggles") after periapsis passage. Previous works are unable to tie the frequency of the wiggles to the orbit, and argue they cannot be quasinormal modes (QNMs). We show that they are Kerr QNMs belonging to the damped-mode branch --- that is, modes whose decay rate stays finite as the spin approaches extremality. For an equatorial prograde orbit with $p = 3M$ and $e = 0.85$ and spins ranging from $a/M = 0.999$ to $a/M = 0.99999$, fits to the time-domain shear identify the fundamental $(\ell,m) = (2,1)$ and $(3,2)$ modes as the dominant and first subdominant excitations. Fitting each multipole separately gives frequencies to within two standard deviations of the Kerr values and provides evidence for the excitation of the fundamental $(3,1)$, $(4,1)$ and $(4,2)$ modes, though these are not resolvable in the full signal. We further argue that the same wiggles must be present in the tidal field that drives the shear. For $\ell \leq 4$ we find no evidence of QNM excitation in the $m = 0$ or the $m = \ell$ multipoles.

Eccentricity-Modulated Phase Degeneracy and Distinguishability between Dark Matter and Accretion Disk Environmental Effects in EMRIs

Tian-Hao Wu, Shu-Jun Rong

Gravitational waves EMRIs Environmental effects

2607.09214v3 · gr-qc

First submitted: 10 Jul 2026 · Last updated: 28 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs; Environmental effects

Read the author-provided abstract

Extreme mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) are sensitive probes of weak environmental effects around massive black holes, since such effects can accumulate into observable gravitational-wave phase shifts. In this work, we study the phase degeneracy between dark matter halos and accretion disks in eccentric EMRI waveforms. We model the dark matter (DM) environment with NFW and Beta halo profiles, and describe the disk using a thin $α$-disk model. Their distinguishability is quantified through eccentricity-dependent phase diagnostics and the residual signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in the LISA band. Our results show that DM-induced dephasing depends only weakly on the initial eccentricity $e_0$, whereas disk-induced dephasing is strongly suppressed as $e_0$ increases. The distinguishability time exhibits a smooth and weakly non-monotonic dependence on the initial eccentricity, while being more strongly controlled by the environmental strength. For the benchmark systems considered here, the DM--disk waveform difference can be detectable by LISA, and $e_0$ can serve as an auxiliary diagnostic in addition to the observation duration.

Resonant transmission of scalar waves through rotating traversable wormhole

Rajesh Karmakar, Bum-Hoon Lee, Wonwoo Lee

Gravitational waves Resonance Quasi-normal modes

2605.09426v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 10 May 2026 · Last updated: 30 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

The viability of traversable wormholes as exotic compact objects relies on identifying signatures that distinguish them from black holes and other compact objects. In this work, we investigate the propagation of a massless scalar field in a rotating traversable wormhole described by Teo's class of solutions. We compute the greybody factor (transmissivity) and identify sharp Breit-Wigner-type resonances associated with scalar modes trapped in the potential well surrounding the throat. Rotation modifies these resonances through frame dragging, with counter rotating modes developing deeper trapping wells. We further investigate the stability of the long lived resonant modes through their quasinormal mode spectrum and find that corotating modes exhibit ergoregion instability. We therefore focus on the partial transmission cross section of the stable counter rotating modes, which characterizes the resonant transmission of incident scalar flux through the wormhole to the other asymptotic region. Our findings establish the role of rotation and mode stability in shaping the resonant transmission of scalar waves through traversable wormholes, providing a basis for future studies of analogous resonant phenomena in electromagnetic and gravitational waves, and their potential observational implications.

Beyond quasinormal modes: a complete mode decomposition of black hole perturbations

Paolo Arnaudo, Javier Carballo, Benjamin Withers

Quasi-normal modes Black-hole perturbation theory

2510.18956v2 · gr-qc, hep-th

First submitted: 21 Oct 2025 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Black-hole perturbation theory

Read the author-provided abstract

We show that retarded Green's functions of black hole spacetimes can be expressed as a convergent mode sum everywhere in spacetime. At late times a quasinormal mode sum converges, while at early times a Matsubara (or, Euclidean) mode sum converges. The two regions are separated by a lightcone which scatters from the black hole potential. The Matsubara sum is a Fourier series on the Euclidean thermal circle associated to the early time region. We illustrate our results for Pöschl-Teller, BTZ, and Schwarzschild. In the case of Schwarzschild, we express the branch cut contribution as a convergent sum of de Sitter quasinormal modes as $Λ\to 0^+$, and exploit recent exact solutions to the Heun connection problem. In each case we analytically show convergence by studying the asymptotics of residue sums and also provide numerical demonstrations.

Pole Skipping, Avoided Crossing, and Resonant Excitation in Kerr Quasinormal Modes near Algebraically Special Frequencies

Kei-ichiro Kubota, Hayato Motohashi

First submitted: 18 May 2026 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

Kerr quasinormal modes near algebraically special frequencies exhibit anomalous behavior, including apparent bifurcation, disappearance, and a nonsmooth connection to the Schwarzschild limit, which has remained puzzling for decades. Tracking poles and zeros of Green-function building blocks across different Riemann sheets, we show that the bifurcation is due to an avoided crossing accompanied by resonant excitation, while the disappearance is due to pole skipping caused by cancellation of a quasinormal-mode pole by a Matsubara-mode zero. This resolves the physical origin of these long-standing anomalies.

SPLIT: Loosely-coherent inference of long-inspiral gravitational-wave sources for robustness against modeling and data outliers

Shubham Kejriwal

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2606.13728v3 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM

First submitted: 11 Jun 2026 · Last updated: 02 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

The Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will detect gravitational waves (GWs) from dozens of extreme- and intermediate-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs/IMRIs). These sources will stay in-band for months to years, offering extraordinary scientific potential. However, their fully phase-coherent analysis standard in current pipelines imposes stringent waveform accuracy requirements; failing to model the signal over such long durations can result in significant systematic biases. To address this, we formulate a robust Bayesian inference framework that segments the data into blocks, infers each block coherently, and recombines the results using loosely-coherent phase information. By restricting full phase-tracking to much shorter block durations, this approach prevents significant accumulation of phase errors. We implement this methodology in \textsc{SPLIT} (Segmented Posteriors for Long-Inspiral Templates), a GPU-accelerated Python package. Applying \textsc{SPLIT} to an environment-rich injection, we demonstrate that while a fully-coherent vacuum-GR analysis incurs a maximum 1D systematic bias of $\approx 4.8σ$ from the truth, the shorter integration window of our loosely-coherent approach restricts such biases to $\lesssim 0.5σ$. Overall, despite a fractional loss of optimal signal-to-noise ratio, the substantial improvement in parameter accuracy offered by our method presents a highly advantageous trade-off for LISA and other future GW detectors.

On the mapping between bound states and black hole quasinormal modes via analytic continuation: a spectral instability perspective

Guan-Ru Li, Wei-Liang Qian, Xiao-Mei Kuang, Ramin G. Daghigh, Jodin C. Morey, Michael D. Green, Peng Xu, Rui-Hong Yue

Quasi-normal modes Black-hole perturbation theory

2603.29704v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 31 Mar 2026 · Last updated: 30 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Black-hole perturbation theory

Read the author-provided abstract

In this work, we investigate the relation between bound states and quasinormal modes within black hole perturbation theory in the context of spectral instability. Our analysis indicates that the reliability of such spectral mapping stretches beyond the domain of validity of the analytic continuation employed to connect the perturbative bound-state problem to the corresponding open-system dynamics. However, for the numerical scheme proposed by Völkel to work, the transformations of the metric parameters must be carried out in a region where the underlying Taylor expansion is convergent. As analytically accessible explicit examples, we explore the perturbed delta-function and Pöschl-Teller potential barriers. For the latter, we construct two distinct perturbative setups for which the convergence of the series expansion involved in the perturbation theory can be rigorously controlled. When the deformation is placed near the potential's extremum, the resulting corrections to the bound-state energies can be analytically continued to yield perturbed quasinormal frequencies, in agreement with known semi-analytic results. In contrast, when the perturbation is localized asymptotically far from the compact object, the bound states are only mildly modified and are accurately described by a perturbative expansion to the first order. However, the associated analytic continuation yields a strongly deformed spectrum that shows no clear connection to the quasinormal modes. These findings contribute to the effort to scrutinize the conditions under which bound states faithfully encode quasinormal spectra and to shed light on the underlying physics of black hole spectral instability.

An efficient approach to resistive GRMHD simulations of binary neutron star mergers

Miquel Miravet-Tenés, Ian Hawke, Rahime Matur, Alex J. Wright

Gravitational waves GRMHD

2609.12998v2 · astro-ph.HE, gr-qc

First submitted: 11 Sep 2026 · Last updated: 30 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; GRMHD

Read the author-provided abstract

Astrophysical plasmas are often highly conducting, but finite resistivity can nevertheless influence the evolution of strongly magnetised systems. Capturing these effects in general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics is computationally challenging because high conductivity leads to stiff source terms in the evolution equations. Standard numerical approaches rely on implicit-explicit (IMEX) time integration to maintain stability, but this substantially increases the complexity and computational cost of the simulations. Building upon the previously developed Resistive Extension upGrade for Ideal MagnEtohydrodynamics (REGIME), we present a new fluid-frame formulation that simplifies the incorporation of leading-order resistive effects by avoiding matrix inversions required in previous approaches. The method is implemented in a general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics code and validated through one-dimensional current-sheet tests and simulations of isolated magnetised neutron stars and binary neutron star mergers. We find that the new formulation reproduces the expected resistive behaviour with accuracy comparable to IMEX-based methods, while substantially reducing the computational cost and complexity of the simulations. In binary neutron star mergers, finite resistivity leads to measurable differences in the post-merger gravitational-wave signal and remnant structure, showing that resistive effects can produce measurable changes in the post-merger dynamics even in the high-conductivity regime.

Quasinormal modes of thick branes in $f(R)$ gravity

Yu-Peng E, Chun-Chun Zhu, Yu-Xiao Liu

Resonance Quasi-normal modes

2512.17208v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 19 Dec 2025 · Last updated: 30 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

We systematically investigate the quasinormal modes of thick branes in $f(R)$ gravity by numerically solving the Schrödinger-like perturbation equation of gravitational perturbations. To ensure the reliability of the results, we employ three complementary methods: the asymptotic iteration method, the direct integration of the wave equation, and the time-domain numerical evolution. We analyze how the model parameters influence the shape of the effective potential of gravitational perturbations and find that the structure of the potential barrier plays a significant role in shaping the quasinormal frequency spectrum. The results obtained from the three methods exhibit strong consistency, thereby ensuring the reliability of the calculations. In particular, the real parts of the quasinormal frequencies exhibit an approximately arithmetic progression, suggesting that the quasi-localized states can be understood as resonances between the barriers.

Constraining polymerized black holes with quasi-circular extreme mass-ratio inspirals

Sen Yang, Yu-Peng Zhang, Tao Zhu, Li Zhao, Yu-Xiao Liu

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2412.04302v3 · gr-qc

First submitted: 05 Dec 2024 · Last updated: 30 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

In this paper, we focus on the gravitational waves emitted by a stellar-mass object in a quasi-circular inspiral orbit around a central supermassive polymerized black hole in loop quantum gravity. Treating the stellar-mass object as a massive test particle, we derive its equations of motion and the corresponding radial effective potential. We find that the peak of the radial effective potential decreases with the quantum parameter $\hat{k}$. We also examine the impact of quantum corrections on the properties of stable circular orbits around the polymerized black hole. We model the smaller object's trajectory as an adiabatic evolution along stable circular orbits using a semi-relativistic approach. In this method, the motion of the object is described by relativistic geodesics, and changes in the object's energy and orbital angular momentum due to gravitational radiation are calculated using the mass quadrupole formula. The corresponding gravitational waveforms are generated using the numerical kludge method, revealing that quantum corrections cause phase advances in the gravitational waveforms. We further analyze the potential constraints on the quantum parameter $\hat{k}$ from future space-based gravitational wave observations, concluding that these observations will likely impose stronger constraints on $\hat{k}$ than those obtained from black hole shadow measurements.

Signatures of Quantum-Corrected Black Holes in Gravitational Waves from Periodic Orbits

Fazlay Ahmed, Qiang Wu, Sushant G Ghosh, Tao Zhu

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2512.24036v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 30 Dec 2025 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

Focusing on a Schwarzschild spacetime modified by loop quantum gravity (LQG) holonomy corrections, this work explores the gravitational radiation produced by test masses in bound periodic configurations. The underlying geometry incorporates a quantum-correction parameter $q_c$ that alters the radial metric scaling while maintaining both asymptotic flatness and the classical horizon radius. The bound geodesics are systematically classified using the zoom--whirl representation labelled by three integers $(z,w,v)$. Gravitational waveforms are computed within a numerical framework that combines exact geodesic motion with the quadrupole approximation, which is suitable for extreme mass ratio inspirals. We demonstrate that the quantum corrections lead to distinct phase shifts, amplitude variations, and modifications to the harmonic structure of the waveforms, with increasingly complex features for orbits with larger zoom numbers. The corresponding frequency spectra and characteristic strain peak, which fall within the millihertz band, are within the sensitivity ranges of space-based detectors such as LISA, Taiji, and TianQin. For specific orbital configurations and values of the quantum-correction parameter, the characteristic strain exceeds the projected detector noise, indicating potential observability. Our results demonstrate that gravitational waves from periodic orbits provide a sensitive probe of quantum-corrected black hole spacetimes in the strong-field regime.

Gravitational-Wave Signatures and Periodic Orbits of Charged Particles Around a Five-Dimensional Rotating Black Hole: Comparison with the Kerr Black Hole

Bahromjon Shokirov, Tursunali Xamidov, Chen Zhou, Sanjar Shaymatov, Chengxun Yuan

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2610.35386v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 26 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

We study periodic orbits of charged particles in the equatorial plane of a five-dimensional ($D=5$) rotating Myers--Perry black hole (BH) immersed in a uniform magnetic field, and the gravitational waves emitted by these orbits in an extreme mass-ratio inspiral. All results are compared with the Kerr BH in the uniform magnetic field. We find that a charged particle is much more weakly bound to the $D=5$ dimensional BH, i.e. its energy at the innermost stable circular orbit is close to unity, $0.95$--$0.99$, compared with $0.78$--$0.94$ for the Kerr BH, and the depth of the potential well is several times smaller. As a result, the periodic orbits of the $D=5$ dimensional BH lie much closer to the separatrix. The magnetic field can make the azimuthal frequency of the orbit smaller than the radial one and produces loops in the orbits far from the BH. Using the numerical kludge method, we compute the gravitational waveforms for a compact object of $10M_\odot$ orbiting a BH of $10^6M_\odot$. At the same magnetic field, the $D=5$ dimensional signal has a longer period, lower frequencies, and an amplitude smaller by about $18\%$. We show that for the magnetic parameter $βM=0.02$ the strongest spectral line is at $7.9$\,mHz, compared with $9.9$\,mHz for the Kerr BH. An estimate of the radiation reaction shows that the periodic orbits survive from less than an hour to a few days in the presence of an external magnetic field. Over these lifetimes, the signal-to-noise ratio for LISA is $7$--$31$ for the rotating BH in $D=5$ dimensions, compared to the Kerr BH where it is about $15$--$54$.

Self-force in hyperbolic black hole encounters

Oliver Long

Self-force Teukolsky

2209.03836v2 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 08 Sep 2022 · Last updated: 29 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Self-force; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

Self-force methods can be applied in calculations of the scatter angle in two-body hyperbolic encounters, working order by order in the mass ratio (assumed small) but with no recourse to a weak-field approximation. This, in turn, can inform ongoing efforts to construct an accurate description of the general-relativistic binary dynamics via an effective-one-body description or other approaches. Existing self-force methods are to a large extent specialised to bound, inspiral orbits. Here we derive the first-order conservative self-force correction to the scattering angle, show its agreement with recent post-Minkowsian results, and develop a technique for (numerical) self-force calculations that can efficiently tackle scatter orbits. In the method, the metric perturbation is reconstructed from a Hertz potential that satisfies (mode-by-mode) a certain inhomogeneous version of the Teukolsky equation. The crucial ingredient in this formulation are certain jump conditions that the (multipole modes of the) Hertz potential must satisfy along the worldline of the small body's orbit. We present a closed-form expression for these jumps, for an arbitrary geodesic orbit in Schwarzschild spacetime. To begin developing the numerical infrastructure, a scalar-field evolution code on a Schwarzschild background (in 1+1D) is developed. Following this, results for the conservative scalar self-force corrections to the scatter angle are calculated. We continue by constructing a Teukolsky evolution code on a Schwarzschild background. This produces numerically unstable solutions due to unphysical homogeneous solutions of the Teukolsky equation at the horizon and null infinity being seeded by numerical error. This can be resolved by a change of variables to a Regge-Wheeler-like field. We then present a full numerical implementation of this method for circular and scatter orbits in Schwarzschild.
