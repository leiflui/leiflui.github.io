Modeling extreme mass-ratio inspirals in astrophysical and dark-matter environments

Dongjun Li, Hao-Jui Kuan, Colin Weller, Chaoyi Yang, Kelvin Ka-Ho Lam, Patrick Bourg, Adrian Ka-Wai Chung, Yanbei Chen, Nicolás Yunes, Huan Yang

Gravitational waves EMRIs Environmental effects Black-hole perturbation theory Teukolsky Collaborator: Adrian Ka-Wai Chung Collaborator: Nicholas Yunes Collaborator: Dongjun Li Collaborator: Kelvin Ka Ho Lam Notable author: Huan Yang Notable author: Yanbei Chen

2610.22480v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 15 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 15 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs; Environmental effects; Black-hole perturbation theory; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

Extreme mass-ratio inspirals offer a powerful probe of massive black holes and their environments, but interpreting their signals requires accurate models of orbital dynamics and radiation. Here we bring together advances in black-hole perturbation theory to establish a unified relativistic framework for adiabatic inspirals in perturbative astrophysical environments. Built on the modified Teukolsky formalism, the framework requires neither post-Newtonian nor slow-rotation approximations. As a first application, we calculate the leading cloud-induced corrections to gravitational-wave fluxes and conservative orbital dynamics for circular, equatorial inspirals around a rotating black hole surrounded by a synchronized ultralight scalar cloud, combining these corrections with the previously calculated scalar response. In our fiducial system, a cloud containing $10^{-4}$ of the black-hole mass produces about $-57.3$ radians of orbital dephasing over two years. The phase contributions from gravitational-wave flux corrections and background-induced orbital shifts each exceed the scalar contribution in magnitude and partially cancel, highlighting the need for coupled matter-gravity modeling. Extensible to other environments and modified gravity theories, our framework provides the relativistic inputs needed to assess detection losses and parameter biases, and to probe the nature of black-hole environments with future space-based gravitational-wave observations.

Periodic orbits and gravitational-wave emission from extreme-mass-ratio inspirals around rotating charged black holes in nonlinear electrodynamics

Xi Luo, Meirong Tang, Zhaoyi Xu

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2610.26113v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 19 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 19 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

Within general-relativistic descriptions, the no-hair theorem dictates that black-hole geometries possess just three intrinsic physical quantities: mass, angular momentum, and electric charge. Black holes in nonlinear electrodynamics (NED) are equivalent to those composed of anisotropic matter and can develop "hair" structures near the event horizon, serving as ideal testbeds for modified theories of gravity. Working within Einsteinian gravitational theory, we adopt a coupling construction between gravity and NED matter, focusing explicitly on the Einstein-Euler-Heisenberg (EEH) variant with the power-index parameter fixed at s = 2. In this framework, a rotating, charged NED black hole extends the Kerr-Newman geometry with extra correction terms. Treating spin a and NED charge q as primary control variables, we examine test-particle dynamics inside extreme-mass-ratio inspiral (EMRI) configurations. We derive equatorial timelike geodesics and the effective potential. Through numerical evaluation of marginally-bound orbits (MBO) and innermost stable circular orbits (ISCO), we explore how these parameters reshape the energy-angular-momentum (E-L) phase-space. Using the (z, w, v) integer-valued orbit taxonomy, we compute the two gravitational-wave polarization modes h+ and hx via the adiabatic approximation and quadrupole radiation prescription. Larger values for a and q drive inward contraction for bound trajectories while displacing the E-L phase-space toward smaller angular-momentum values. Periodic-orbit zoom-whirl signatures grow increasingly prominent, while larger parameter values shorten the waveform periods and enhance signal amplitudes. Our results provide theoretical support for space-borne gravitational-wave observatories including LISA and TianQin, enabling prospects for discriminating NED black-hole spacetimes and performing observational checks on the no-hair theorem.

Conservative Orbital Resonance Networks as Local Environmental Diagnostics for Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals

Mona Kumari, Anirudh Pradhan, K. Ghaderi, Javlon Rayimbaev

EMRIs Resonance

2610.23174v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 16 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 16 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. EMRIs; Resonance

Read the author-provided abstract

LLow order orbital resonances provide localized probes of the conservative geometry sampled by extreme mass ratio inspirals. We study fixed eccentricity and inclination slices of the ordered $3{:}2$, $5{:}3$, and $2{:}1$ polar to radial resonance surfaces in source consistent anisotropic black hole environments, treating rotation strictly to first order. A horizon regularized Dehnen sequence produces unequal resonance displacements, reaching $-7.17\%$, $-5.45\%$, and $-3.94\%$ in a fixed global benchmark. After matching both the total environmental mass and the enclosed mass at $10M_\bullet$, we compare Dehnen and Einasto inspired families. Two dimensionless network spacing ratios are organized primarily by the local logarithmic enclosed mass slope: for the common $β=2$ regularization their maximum cross family departure from a shared linear relation is $4.1\times10^{-4}$, while varying the horizon taper through $β=2$--$4$ increases the departure to at most $2.1\times10^{-3}$. The unequal ordering of the three resonance displacements holds at every point of a directly evaluated finite eccentricity and inclination grid. A six observable conservative response analysis is numerically full rank but strongly ill conditioned and nearly aligned with orbital nuisance directions. The result therefore establishes local conservative profile sensitivity, not unique tomography or a detector level parameter recovery forecast.

Biases in Ringdown Tests of General Relativity from Gravitational-Wave Microlensing

Sreelakshmi Mundakkal Dasan, Anuj Mishra, Apratim Ganguly

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing Quasi-normal modes

2610.20201v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 14 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

Gravitational-wave (GW) ringdown signals provide a powerful probe of the strong-field regime of general relativity (GR) through black hole spectroscopy and tests of the Kerr hypothesis. However, unmodeled propagation effects can bias the inference of remnant properties and the interpretation of ringdown features. We investigate the impact of gravitational lensing on ringdown inference and tests of GR using GW150914-like simulated signals spanning the long-wavelength, wave-optics, and geometric-optics regimes. We perform complementary time-domain post-merger and frequency-domain full inspiral--merger--ringdown analyses using unlensed waveform models. We find that lensing in the wave-optics regime can produce substantial biases in the inferred remnant mass and spin and can strongly affect the inferred modal content of the ringdown. In particular, it can spuriously favor the presence of the first overtone, with $\log_{10}\mathcal{B}^{221}_{220}\sim40$, even though the injected signal has no support for it. Parameterized ringdown tests can likewise yield strong apparent support for deviations from the Kerr quasinormal mode (QNM) spectrum, with $\log_{10}\mathcal{B}^{\rm modGR}_{\rm GR}\sim8$. In contrast, the evidence for higher-order modes remains largely unchanged for the simulated signals considered here. The parameterized analysis using the entire signal further shows that microlensing can bias the inferred frequency of the dominant $(2,2,0)$ QNM. These results demonstrate that unmodeled microlensing can produce systematic biases and spurious signatures in ringdown analyses, potentially affecting precision tests of the Kerr hypothesis.

Detectability of Gravitational Waves from Extreme-Mass-Ratio Inspirals on Periodic Orbits around Bardeen Black Holes

Li Huang, Xing He

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2610.19521v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 14 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

We investigate gravitational wave (GW) signatures from extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) around a Bardeen black hole, modeling the small object as a test particle, and provide a framework for probing strong-field spacetime geometry. We first present the equations of motion for test particles around a Bardeen black hole and characterize circular orbits in the parameter space of energy and angular momentum. Using Levin's classification, we adopt the extended classification scheme to classify periodic orbits around a Bardeen black hole. We then compute GW waveforms in both the time and frequency domains, as well as the corresponding characteristic strains, for these periodic orbits and compare them with the sensitivity curves of the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA), the evolved Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (eLISA), the TianQin mission, the Big Bang Observer (BBO), and the Decihertz Interferometer Gravitational wave Observatory (DECIGO). We find that detectability is controlled primarily by the luminosity distance. At a luminosity distance of 200 Mpc, the signals can exceed the sensitivity curves of BBO and DECIGO and are comparable to the LISA sensitivity curve, whereas at 2 Gpc, they remain below the sensitivity curves of all detectors considered. These results suggest that EMRIs on periodic orbits around Bardeen black holes offer a promising avenue to probe strong-field gravity and the properties of Bardeen black holes.

Searching for lensed gravitational waves with unlensed templates

Digvijay Wadekar, Barak Zackay, Mark Ho-Yeuk Cheung, Tousif Islam, Javier Roulet, Ajit Kumar Mehta, Tejaswi Venumadhav, Jonathan Mushkin, Miguel Zumalacarregui, Matias Zaldarriaga

Gravitational waves Gravitational lensing

2610.26080v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.IM

First submitted: 19 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 19 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Gravitational lensing

Read the author-provided abstract

Gravitational lensing can split the signal of a binary merger into two images, which either overlap and interfere or arrive as separate events. We show that the matched-filter output of every two-image template can be straightforwardly constructed from the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) time series that searches already compute with unlensed templates. This enables a marginalized detection statistic that increases search sensitivity without additional matched filtering and is more than 150 times faster than an explicit lensed template bank. For overlapping images in the geometric-optics regime, our two-image method recovers $\gtrsim97\%$ of the optimal SNR from point-mass and singular isothermal lenses, compared with as little as $67\%$ captured by unlensed templates, with substantial gains also for Chang-Refsdal and cored isothermal lenses. Overlapping lensed signals moreover fail the signal-consistency test that is used to down-rank noise transients. Our two-image method restores the consistency and increases the sensitive volume by roughly $2$-$4\times$ for the strongly down-ranked signals. For separated images, our method enables an efficient coherent search for pairs in which neither image is significant alone. Near fold caustics, this increases the sensitive volume by factors of 1.8-2.6 over requiring two independent detections, and up to 1.6 over a targeted counterpart search. Our method is applicable to any matched-filtering search.

Probing parity-breaking black-hole geometry with extreme-mass-ratio inspirals

Zhen-Bo Wu, Qing Ou, Peng-Cheng Li

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2610.25291v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 18 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 18 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

We investigate the gravitational-wave signatures of equatorial reflection symmetry breaking in extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) around a parity-violating conformal Kerr black hole. For the circular-orbit branch connected to equatorial Kerr orbits, we derive the dynamics through second order in the dimensionless deformation parameter $\varepsilon$ and construct adiabatic inspirals and gravitational-wave signals using a mass-quadrupole prescription. Equatorial reflection maps $\varepsilon$ to $-\varepsilon$, making the polar displacement odd and the orbital frequency and energy even in the deformation. The induced first harmonic has a small direct contribution for the configurations studied, while second-order dynamical corrections accumulate over many cycles to produce appreciable dephasing. We find that rapid black-hole rotation enhances these phase effects and the associated noise-weighted waveform mismatch. For a one-year inspiral of a $10M_\odot$ object into a $10^6M_\odot$ black hole with $a/M=0.9$ and viewing inclination $ι=2π/3$, the fixed-parameter mismatch reaches the comparison benchmark $\mathcal M=0.01$ at approximately $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, without optimization over time, phase, or Kerr parameters. A single-parameter Fisher analysis also indicates improved local sensitivity to $\varepsilon$ at high spin. These results connect the reflection properties of the circular branch to accumulated source-phase effects and complement tests of propagation birefringence. The sensitivity estimates remain conditional on the adiabatic quadrupole model and the adopted comparison procedure; robust observational constraints require more accurate radiation modeling and a full parameter-estimation analysis.

Read the author-provided abstract

Based on black-hole perturbation-theory calculations and an effective-one-body picture of binary black hole mergers, it has been suggested that a direct wave may be present and measurable in the ringdown. Furthermore, it has been suggested that this signal might originate from substantially inside the light ring (or photon sphere) of the remnant black hole, perhaps even from close to the event horizon. In this paper, we present three independent strands of evidence against such claims. In the process, we show that there is indeed an early-time part of the ringdown signal that cannot be directly modeled by quasinormal modes. However, there is no evidence that this is a direct wave as it has previously been defined, and we show that the properties of this component are related to the remnant's light ring rather than its horizon.

Towards ultrarelativistic scattering near the light ring: asymptotic divergence of gravitational-wave fluxes

David Trestini, Riccardo Gonzo, Benjamin Leather

Self-force Gravitational waves Notable author: David Trestini

2610.20708v1 · gr-qc, hep-th

First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 14 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Self-force; Gravitational waves

Read the author-provided abstract

Near the threshold between scattering and capture by a Schwarzschild black hole, a particle spends a long time close to an unstable circular orbit, which in the ultrarelativistic limit approaches the light ring. Here we study the behavior of the gravitational wave fluxes emitted by such an ultrarelativistic particle at first order in the mass ratio. The fluxes radiated to infinity and absorbed by the horizon diverge as the inverse distance from the light ring multiplied by its logarithm. Using the Wentzel, Kramers, and Brillouin (WKB) approximation to solve the Regge-Wheeler equation and treating the infinite harmonic sum analytically, we derive an exact expression for the coefficient of this logarithmic enhancement and show that it is identical for both fluxes. We then estimate the coefficients of the subleading pole contributions by combining numerical calculations at low multipoles with the exact asymptotics at large multipoles. We further conjecture an extension to equatorial circular orbits in Kerr. Interestingly, our results also inform the analytical structure of the energy and angular momentum losses of ultrarelativistic scattering orbits near the light ring. More broadly, our work illustrates how WKB methods can provide the asymptotic behavior of self-force observables in regimes where numerical mode sums become increasingly challenging.

Cubic Quasinormal Modes in the Black-Hole Ringdown

Neev Khera, Huan Yang

Quasi-normal modes Teukolsky Notable author: Huan Yang

2610.20547v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Last updated: 14 Oct 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

We formulate and numerically solve the nonlinear Teukolsky equations through third order, obtaining a first-principles calculation of cubic quasinormal-mode amplitudes for Kerr black holes. Our approach combines lower-order metric reconstruction in split light-cone gauges with a hyperboloidal, compactified formulation solved on complex radial contours. Spin-dependent complex coupling coefficients fix the cubic amplitudes from products of their linear parent amplitudes. We apply these predictions to a nonprecessing numerical-relativity waveform, including cubic products of the $(2,2)$ fundamental mode, its first overtone, and their conjugates.
