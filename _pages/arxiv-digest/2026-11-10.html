Kerr metric perturbations for second-order self-force theory

Benjamin Leather, Samuel D. Upton, Patrick Bourg, Kevin O. Cunningham, Sam R. Dolan, Conor Dyson, Zachary Nasipak, Nami Nishimura, Andrew Spiers, Maarten van de Meent, Barry Wardell

Self-force Environmental effects Quasi-normal modes Teukolsky Notable author: Maarten van de Meent Notable author: Conor Dyson Notable author: Benjamin Leather

2611.03888v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 02 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 02 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Self-force; Environmental effects; Quasi-normal modes; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

We present a calculation of the first-order metric perturbation arising from a point mass moving on a circular, equatorial orbit in Kerr spacetime. The metric perturbation is specifically aimed at meeting the requirements of second-order self-force theory: it is highly accurate; incorporates all contributions, including those associated with perturbations to the black hole's mass and angular momentum; is in Lorenz gauge near the worldline and in (near) Bondi--Sachs gauge at large radius; and can be efficiently evaluated across the entire exterior spacetime. The Lorenz gauge gives a well-understood singular structure at the particle, while the (near) Bondi--Sachs gauge ensures the asymptotic fall-off needed for an asymptotically flat second-order source. Our formalism reconstructs the metric from separable Teukolsky-type equations, decomposed into spin-weighted spheroidal harmonic modes and solved spectrally on hyperboloidal slices. The resulting $(\ell,m)$-mode sums converge rapidly across most of the domain, but slowly near the point mass; we overcome this with a novel use of a puncture to extract accurate $m$-mode data, yielding output accurate to $\sim 10^{-10}$ per $m$-mode and suitable for use at second order. Our metric-perturbation results cover a range of Kerr spins $-0.9 \le a/M \le 0.9$ and orbital radii out to $50M$. In addition to its immediate application in second-order self-force calculations, our results can also be used as input to other calculations involving Kerr metric perturbations, including studies of environmental effects and quasinormal modes.

Multi-domain spectral method for Kerr spacetimes: a hyperboloidal Teukolsky $m$-mode solver for self-force computations

Brad Cownden, Rodrigo Panosso Macedo, Christiana Pantelidou, Samuel D. Upton, Barry Wardell

Self-force Black-hole perturbation theory Teukolsky

2611.03870v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 02 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 02 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Self-force; Black-hole perturbation theory; Teukolsky

Read the author-provided abstract

We solve the frequency-domain, $m$-mode Teukolsky equation for the spin-weight $s=-2$ Weyl scalar $ψ_4$ sourced by a point mass on a circular, equatorial orbit around a Kerr black hole. The equation is posed on hyperboloidal slices that reach the event horizon and future null infinity, so that the physical (retarded) solution is selected by regularity alone, without imposing radiation conditions. The particle is treated with the $m$-mode puncture/effective-source scheme, and the resulting two-dimensional elliptic problem is solved with a four-domain pseudo-spectral method in which the equatorial symmetry is broken and handled by an even/odd decomposition of the field. We test the method for orbital radius $r_0=10M$ and spins up to $|a|/M=0.9$, covering prograde and retrograde orbits. The energy fluxes to infinity agree with independent Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit results to $\sim10^{-7}$ for the dominant modes and to better than $2\times10^{-5}$ for all modes up to $m=20$ at moderate resolutions. The static $m=0$ mode requires no special treatment.

Research on gravitational waves from extreme mass ratio inspirals in an axisymmetric Finslerian extension of Schwarzschild spacetime

Jin-Tao Yao, Zhi-Chao Zhao, Xin Li

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2611.06018v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 04 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 04 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

We investigate gravitational waves from extreme mass ratio inspirals in an axisymmetric Finslerian extension of the Schwarzschild spacetime. Its angular component is described by a two-dimensional Randers space, with the Schwarzschild limit recovered at Finslerian parameter $ε= 0$. This structure breaks spherical symmetry, causing generic orbits to undergo orbital plane precession. Using multipolar radiation reaction and adiabatic orbital averaging, we derive the leading order Finslerian corrections to the energy, axial angular momentum, and total angular momentum fluxes. These corrections enter at Newtonian order and take different forms. We then incorporate these corrections into a higher-order Schwarzschild hybrid flux model to evolve the orbital elements and construct waveforms at mass quadrupole order using the numerical kludge method. The Finslerian corrections affect both the conservative orbital dynamics and the dissipative inspiral, producing cumulative phase shifts relative to the Schwarzschild waveform. For the representative source observed by LISA over one year with signal-to-noise ratio of 20, the noise weighted mismatch analysis shows that Finslerian waveforms with $|ε| \gtrsim 2 \times 10^{-6}$ are distinguishable from their Schwarzschild counterparts, with higher initial eccentricities further lowering the distinguishable value of $|ε|$. For a Schwarzschild injection, the Fisher matrix analysis yields a marginalized $1σ$ uncertainty of $σ_ε\simeq 1.26 \times 10^{-6}$. These results show the potential of space-based gravitational wave detectors to probe the axisymmetric Finslerian extension of the Schwarzschild spacetime.

Black hole quasinormal mode excitation and mixing for coupled fields

Caio F. B. Macedo, Orival R. de Medeiros, Emanuele Berti, Hector O. Silva, Sebastian H. Völkel

Gravitational waves Resonance Quasi-normal modes

2611.05488v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 03 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 03 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Resonance; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

The idea of testing the nature of black holes and possible deviations from general relativity from their oscillation spectrum, known as ``black hole spectroscopy'', has become an experimental science. In many modified theories of gravity, the gravitational perturbations couple to additional degrees of freedom, leading to mode mixing and to a more complex ringdown signal. We adopt the parametrized quasinormal mode formalism for nonrotating black holes to study coupled perturbations in modified gravity theories in order to understand how the interaction with additional fields affects the quasinormal mode frequencies and their amplitudes. We compute the quasinormal mode spectrum of the coupled system in the frequency domain using direct integration and a matrix-valued continued-fraction method. We then compare the results with time-domain evolutions, where we extract the mode frequencies and amplitudes using the Prony method as well as least-squares fitting. In the weak-coupling regime, the scaling of the amplitudes associated with the gravitational-led and secondary-led modes can be understood perturbatively. The excitation of additional families of ringdown modes can become significant even when the frequency shifts remain moderate. We use our numerical time evolutions to provide ready-to-use coefficients for the mode amplitudes for representative initial data configurations. We work out specific examples, including dynamical Chern-Simons gravity and the special near-resonant case of coupled perturbations between the polar and axial quasinormal modes. Our theory-agnostic characterization of coupled systems should be useful to quantify systematic effects in black hole spectroscopy with next-generation gravitational-wave detectors.

Quasinormal Modes of Black holes in the generalized Schwarzschild-de Sitter solution of $f(R)$ gravity

Dhruba Jyoti Gogoi, Himanshu Chaudhary, Tiberiu Harko

Gravitational waves Quasi-normal modes

2611.11895v1 · gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

First submitted: 09 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 09 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

We analyze scalar, vector, and {axial} tensor perturbations in a Schwarzschild-type static spherically symmetric solution of modified $f(R)$ gravity. This solution differs from the Schwarzschild metric of general relativity through the presence of a linearly growing term and a logarithmic correction, and it depends on four integration constants. Using the higher-order Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin (WKB) approximation and an analytical method, we calculate the quasinormal modes (QNMs) for all three types of perturbations. Our results show that, for appropriately chosen parameter values, the QNMs closely match those of the Schwarzschild black hole. In particular, the model parameter $A$ produces a nonlinear increase in both the oscillation frequency and damping rate of ringdown gravitational waves. A similar effect arises for the parameter $η$ when $A$ is small, while for larger $A$ values the effects are opposite, where the scalar perturbations display a distinct deviation compared to the vector and {axial} tensor cases. Contour plots of QNMs in the parameter space support these findings. Finally, time-domain profiles are obtained to illustrate the dynamical evolution of the gravitational wave signal.

Equatorial Periodic Orbits and Gravitational Wave Signatures in Weyl Geometric Gravity Black Holes

Dhruba Jyoti Gogoi, Himanshu Chaudhary, Tiberiu Harko

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2611.08952v1 · gr-qc, hep-th

First submitted: 06 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 06 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

Weyl geometric gravity theory is a gravitational theory with Lagrangian density constructed from the square of the Weyl curvature scalar and the strength of the Weyl vector. The theory can be reformulated as a scalar - vector - tensor theory in the Riemann geometry by introducing an auxiliary scalar field, in the presence of a non - minimal coupling between the Ricci scalar and the scalar field. Assuming that the Weyl vector has only a radial component, an exact spherically symmetric black hole type solution of the field equations in the vacuum can be obtained. The solution depends on three arbitrary integration constants. As compared to the case of the Schwarzschild black hole, the Weyl geometric gravity black hole solution contains two new terms, linear and quadratic, in the radial coordinate. It is the main goal of the present work to investigate astrophysical properties of the Weyl geometric black holes by considering the behavior of the timelike geodesics, which allows to explore how the geometric nonmetricity influences the strong-field dynamics of massive test particles as well as the topological structure of the periodic orbits. The resulting gravitational wave emission in extreme mass-ratio inspiral (EMRI) systems is also considered. Our results show that the presence of nonmetricity in Weyl geometric gravity leaves distinct and measurable signatures on both the orbital topology and the emitted gravitational waveforms of compact EMRI systems. These unique temporal and amplitude modulations provide a promising observational avenue for testing the presence of nonmetricity on cosmic scales.

Rational Orbits and Gravitational-Wave Signatures around a Horndeski Black Hole

Wei Gao, Xiaoyan Zhu, Wenbin Lin, Siming Liu

Gravitational waves EMRIs

2611.07596v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 05 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 05 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; EMRIs

Read the author-provided abstract

We study equatorial timelike geodesics and associated gravitational-wave (GW) emission for a static Horndeski black hole with secondary scalar hair, characterized by an effective pressure parameter $p_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Using the rational-orbit taxonomy $(z,w,v)$, we analyze the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO), marginally bound orbit (MBO), and periodic bound orbits. We find that increasing $p_{\mathrm{eff}}$ systematically shifts orbital radii outward and modifies the rational parameter $q$. Within the test-particle and quadrupole approximations, we estimate GW signals from an extreme mass-ratio inspiral (EMRI) system at a luminosity distance of $D_L=8000\,\mathrm{pc}$. The characteristic strains reach $\sim10^{-18}$ in the mHz band, partially overlapping the nominal LISA sensitivity range. These results demonstrate that Horndeski corrections systematically alter strong-field orbital dynamics and GW spectra, offering a phenomenological probe of deviations from general relativity.

Axial black hole perturbations in purely-metric Horndeski theories

Héloïse Delaporte, Hugo Roussille

Quasi-normal modes Black-hole perturbation theory

2611.06875v1 · gr-qc

First submitted: 04 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 04 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Black-hole perturbation theory

Read the author-provided abstract

We study axial (odd-parity) perturbations about static and spherically-symmetric curvaturised black hole solutions of purely-metric Horndeski theories. We first review the construction of purely-metric theories of gravity with second-order equations of motion and an additional propagating scalar degree of freedom which is purely gravitational. We then revisit the formation, in a subclass of these theories, of a new branch of hairy black hole solutions under tachyonic instabilities of the scalar field near the Schwarzschild solution. Because a non-zero Ricci curvature grows and by analogy with black hole scalarisation, the latter black hole formation mechanism is dubbed \emph{curvaturisation}. We then apply the first-order approach to the dynamics of axial perturbations and find that it is equivalent to that of axial perturbations in general relativity evolving in a distinct effective metric. We explicitly provide that effective metric in terms of the background and the Lagrangian functions, and show that it shares the same causality structure as the background metric of curvaturised black holes. By turning to the Schrödinger-like formulation of the perturbations, we analyse the scattering potential and conclude that the axial perturbations of curvaturised black holes are linearly stable. Finally, we confirm these findings by explicitly computing the fundamental quasi-normal mode as well as the first five overtones for the quadrupole $\ell = 2$.

Quasi-Normal Modes and thermalization in integrable Quantum Field Theories from the Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz

Zoltan Bajnok, Romuald A. Janik

Quasi-normal modes Black-hole perturbation theory

2611.06473v1 · hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech, quant-ph

First submitted: 04 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 04 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes; Black-hole perturbation theory

Read the author-provided abstract

We propose a Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz (TBA) approach for describing thermalization in integrable quantum field theories. The exponential damping and oscillations when approaching thermal equilibrium can be understood as the analogs of quasi-normal mode (QNM) frequencies of black hole perturbations within the framework of holography. Holographic intuitions and some general properties of thermal Green's functions motivate the existence of such a TBA formulation, as well as lead to the imposition of a nonstandard analytical structure on the QNM TBA, while comparison with integrable form-factor expansions fixes the remaining ingredients. We conjecture the QNM TBA to be equivalent to performing an all-order resummation of secular terms in a thermal form factor expansion of retarded 2-point correlation functions. We compare the predictions with tensor network simulations of the E8 deformation of Ising CFT at nonzero temperature. We conjecture that the QNM TBA framework should also be applicable for describing out-of-equilibrium approach to Generalized Hydrodynamics (GHD).

Quasi-Normal Modes of Covariant Loop Quantum Black Holes

Chao Zhang, Sushant G. Ghosh, Anzhong Wang

Gravitational waves Quasi-normal modes

2611.03902v1 · gr-qc, hep-th

First submitted: 02 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 02 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves; Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

We investigate the quasinormal modes (QNMs) and time-domain response of a massless, minimally coupled test scalar field on static, spherically symmetric covariant loop quantum black hole (cLQBH) backgrounds. Focusing on a phenomenological, Gaussian-localized holonomy profile, we obtain a localized deformation of the scalar effective potential containing both positive and negative parts. This provides a geometric realization of the bump effect, with the potential deformation and tortoise-coordinate mapping determined consistently by the same background. We characterize the deformation by its location, amplitude, and width, and study its effects using frequency-domain shooting and time-domain evolution, supplemented by matrix-pencil mode extraction and waveform reconstruction. The QNM spectrum exhibits spiral trajectories as the bump location varies, enhanced sensitivity of the overtones, and mode overtaking, whereby the branch continued from the first Schwarzschild overtone becomes longer-lived than the continued fundamental branch. Despite substantial spectral changes, the early response can remain close to its Schwarzschild counterpart, with more pronounced differences emerging later. To quantify this behavior, we introduce a local waveform-deviation time and compare it with a relative-amplitude faintness time, thereby defining an operational observation window. The deviation time depends nonmonotonically on the bump location, and suitable parameter choices allow appreciable departures to emerge before the adopted faintness threshold is reached. These results connect localized holonomy corrections with spectral sensitivity and waveform evolution, providing a basis for future gravitational-perturbation studies and assessments of gravitational-wave detectability.

Excitation factors and exceptional points of Kerr--Newman quasinormal modes

Jun-Kun Zhao, Tieguang Zi

Quasi-normal modes

2611.05201v2 · gr-qc

First submitted: 03 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 09 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Quasi-normal modes

Read the author-provided abstract

We study excitation factors and investigate exceptional points (EPs) of Kerr--Newman quasinormal modes using the exact separated gravitoelectromagnetic equations. At fixed charge, the magnitudes of the excitation factors for the gravitational-led modes studied here decrease with increasing spin, whereas those for electromagnetic-led modes exhibit both monotonic and nonmonotonic behavior. The magnitude of the excitation factor for the first overtone can be comparable to or larger than that for the fundamental mode. We identify 42 EPs at which pairs of modes coalesce in the gravitational-led and electromagnetic-led sectors. Some EPs form sequences involving successive overtone pairs, with higher-overtone pairs coalescing progressively closer to the extremal limit. The additional EPs identified outside these sequences all share the same mode-pair labels as sequence members, but occur at different values of the black hole spin and charge. These findings clarify how rotation and charge shape the relative excitation strengths of different modes and the pattern of mode coalescence in the non-Hermitian spectrum of Kerr--Newman black holes.

Investigating the Viability of Dedicated Microhertz Gravitational Wave Pulsar Timing

C. F. Sheridan, E. F. Keane

Gravitational waves

2611.12179v1 · astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM

First submitted: 09 Nov 2026 · Last updated: 09 Nov 2026 (UTC)

Why included: Topic match. Gravitational waves

Read the author-provided abstract

The microhertz gravitational wave (GW) frequency regime remains one of the least explored regions of the GW spectrum. This is due to the physical limitations of our current and upcoming GW observatories such as LIGO and LISA, which are tuned to dHz and mHz respectively. Proposed experiments targeting the $μ$Hz regime are ambitious, expensive and unlikely to be operational within the next 20 years. Pulsar Timing Arrays (PTAs), with their Galaxy-sized baselines, occupy the other end of the spectrum; the frequencies they are sensitive to are a function of the observing cadence and total dataset timespan. Existing PTA campaigns have cadences and timespans corresponding to the nanohertz regime, however the possibility exists that they can be 'pushed' to detect higher frequencies. This paper investigates the viability of a custom microhertz-sensitive PTA, observing a small number of millisecond pulsars with a daily cadence, over the course of 1 and 2 years respectively. We assume the use of both 100-m and 500-m class radio telescopes for these observations, and an idealised noise model with negligible systematics to determine the most optimistic detection threshold. We present realistic eccentricity distributions for extreme mass ratio inspirals (EMRIs) forming via the two-body relaxation channel in nuclear star clusters, tracking their evolution up to the final plunge onto the central Schwarzschild massive black hole (MBH).
